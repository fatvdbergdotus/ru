## Status of ethical knowledge
As many areas of knowledge, ethics receive a lot of attention in universities. Ethics deal with what is considered morally right and wrong. The ideal scientist should always be involved with this, because the goal of a scientist should be the make the world a better place in the long run. For example when a  scientist introduces new knowledge, its consequences good be either turn out good or bad. But this is when the first problem occurs. It's hard to predict what an invention will do and how it will be used in the future. Also it's often not possible to foreseen what new inventions the original will trigger. For this cause different ethical frameworks have been developed.

The frameworks, which will be referred to a paradigms from now on, provide different measurements for self reflection or for reflecting others' behavior and do not necessary need to lead to the same actions to be taken. This is very unwanted when one assumes there's one moral objective truth. The different frameworks focus on intentions, consequences and heroes as an example in respectively deontologic, utilistic and virtue ethics. And it's not really strange to think in the future there'll be different points of view as well. The problem this arises is because we live in a real time world in which there is limited time to think. For every action we take, which always have moral value, we need to decide how long to think about its justification. Because of the limited time we will always skip information that's relevant for the decision from the moral perspective and therefore always make a less good decision as theoretically and objectively possible.

Let's compare this status with other sciences. For example in mathematics there are sometimes problems that aren't solved as well, but one major difference occurs here. When a problem is solved here it can be proved and its result will be the same until the end of time. For ethics this is very unlikely to be the case and at least hasn't been the case so far. Going to a little less deterministic science physics is reached. Physics has turned out to be useful and many theories from long times ago still seem to hold. However unlike mathematics we're talking about a real world here that hasn't built up from induction steps and therefore in physics theoretically we're just trying to look around a bit and make some structure in our observations. This sounds a bit negative, but the way of working is still much more structured then the case of ethics. One many things in physics there's consensus for a start and also much knowledge developed in physics has proved its value. How would be otherwise be able to drive cars and fly to the moon.


The science area to which ethical knowledge shows more comparison to is the field of AI. In AI there are often different paradigms which all have some aspects that can be considered useful. Despite of knowing the ultimate solution there or one objective way of working that should always work, different paradigms have shown to have some value albeit on limited scale compared to what we'd like to achieve. Ethics seems to suffer from the same problems. Different frameworks are used and even religion is involved for many people, but often we don't know exactly what they are completely based on and were to find to overlap. Generally however, people tend to behave in developed countries and law systems and forms of authority seemed to have developed quite advanced to achieve cooperation between people in something we call economy. Without considering ethics at all this wouldn't have been possible at all.

In the next paragraph the point of the ethics make people better persons and how ethics are actually useful will be discussed more precisely. Also the point of an absolute truth being there will be discussed and its implications.


## Importance of ethical knowledge
How is ethical knowledge important. First of all the ethical frameworks make it possible to make a generalization for actions in a ethical framework. Because of this actions can be compared from a certain point of view and questions such as what should I have done instead or did a do the right thing can be (at least) partly answered. Also ethics make it possible for people to defend themselves and for example show that according a certain framework it's not really bad to take a certain action. An example would be to kill a person that was going to kill a whole community in some way, such as a dictator. Although killing isn't something that's normally easily justified ethically, there could be cases in which people are able to succesfully defend themselves. What can be seen from the just mentioned is that ethical knowledge can help with:
- reflecting on your actions and learn from your mistakes for sake of the future
- help people defend themselves for taking certain actions or understand others better for doing certain things
- control society in going the right direction

All of the just mentioned sounds great at first sight and if ethics are used in the ideal way it would certainly be the case. However there are some problems that occur with ethics that in some case might even turn out to have worse results then not using ethics at all:
- is there an absolute objective truth in morality?
- are there frameworks without flaws?
- after long thinking ethics can be used to justify actions


Firstly the point of a common truth being there with respect to morality. This point can be mentioned because of different reasons. When there's a deterministic certain truth, we already assume that the world is deterministic and that everything can be calculated. This doesn't need to be the case. When this is not the case we can approach a situation that's feasible with respect to morality, but there might always be cases in which it's very very hard to make decisions and in which the wrong decision might be made. 

Next the flaws in frameworks. In case of utilitarianism: For example the case of democracy, which is often used and often seen as a fair way to make decisions: What if 51 out of 100 people agree a little with something and 49 out of 100 people very very strongly are against it. In that case democracy will still favor for the majority while this might not lead to the most wanted situation. In life many of such cases occur which in game theory is being referred to as zero sum games. This means that benefit of one person will lead to disadvantage for another. So even if we'd be able to calculate the total utility for society the highest total utility still doesn't need  to be the best solution, because it might require some people to sacrifice themselves a lot.
In case of theological ethics one can ask who defined the ethics for a start and should we have faith in this. For example 'thou shall not kill' doesn't always need to be a good thing as illustrated before. 
In the cases which intentions and consequences play a role it's hard to figure out which information one has, one should have access to and how long one should have thought about coming to the right conclusions. The last point was mentioned earlier as being a problem of being in a real time world in which there's finite resources and time for taking decisions.

Third the justification of actions. Because of the different frameworks it might be the case that someone purposely does a morally wrong action with perhaps also morally bad consequences, but still get away with it. After doing this action, the person can start investigating different frameworks and look for holes in them. An example is Robin Hood stealing for the poor. Similar things happen on the level of law. Although law and ethics aren't equal they do show some properties that can be compared. In the case of law holes are often exploited and even though every knows that this is something that is not acceptable, people still get away with the worst things.

## Expert computer system
The last paragraph has shown how ethics can be useful and how ethics can flaw on certain areas. In this chapter a fictional computer system is introduced that can make perfect ethical decisions. The feasibility of this system is being mentioned, the limitations that are still there in having it practically, the way it would be accepted by the people and finally a theoretical perspective are mentioned.

Firstly the feasibility of the system. There might be reasons the have and reasons to not have it. A reason to have it is that in some cases it might be worth to know what's good and what's bad. As mentioned in the beginning of the second paragraph ethics can guide in certain areas, such as helping people to become better persons. In this case the ethical advisor can be used to ask what one did wrong to use it for improvement in the future. In contrast to that there are also aspects that can make or make the system unfeasible. First of all the autonomy of people. When the ethical advisor would be used all the time immoral behavior from people wouldn't be tolerated anymore and therefore people would start to behave a certain way as expected from them. In some degree this takes a way their autonomy, because their freedom of movement gets smaller. This is comparable to a part in the movie 'I Robot' based on the laws of Ashimov. In the end the robots start locking people up for their own good, because when they go into the real world they cause problems with each other and basically destroy their own race that way. This is clearly not a situation which humans like to achieve even though it might be morally perfect. Another negative aspect that's closely related to the just mentions and actually takes down the first argument is the way people learn. In our brain learning occurs through conditioning. In practice this means we take a certain action and then see the result of it. If the result of a certain actions is often good, we'll prefer the action in many cases. In case of the ethical advisor being there, we might reach the point in which we're not allowed to go through this feedback loop anymore which means we're not learning anything about right and wrong anymore which in a way which is strongly the case in a baby growing up. This might lead to a situation in which, when the ethical advisor is not available or involved in something else, people are not able to make right decisions, leading to catastrophic result. Metaphorically our dependence on  television is a good illustration (people go crazy when the cable is not working). More on this topic is mentioned in the paper 'Computing machinery and morality' by  Blay withby. However, he also leaves in the middle what way to go and whether such as system is wanted.


Secondly the case in which such a system practically exists. Of course we don't know what it exactly would look like if an ethical advisor would be realized, but the assumption here is that the advisor given a certain input, which includes a world and a decision, returns a value of that decision and perhaps a preferable decision that could be taken. Problems occur when looking at the input. Since a computer is deterministic and finite, there are restrictions on the input. Imagine that the world is deterministic as well or that computers have some way of dealing with non-deterministic situations (for example by using probability theory) the finite part of the input would still be a problem. Since it's not possible to give the whole universe as an input to the computer and since everything depends on everything in the universe (eg. The butterfly effect) one has to assume that the computer has to work with some model of reality. The risk with this is that not all relevant information is at hand, even when the computer can extract the information from the universe itself. This leads to the point in which cannot guarantee that the decision the advisor makes is 100% accurate and the best one can expect. When the quality of the advisor is overestimated or overused this can lead to terrible situations. Even when a small fractions of the decisions is wrong, because the advisor is assumed to be accessed often (eg. imagine a website a la Google taking care of the interface between humans and the advisor).

The just mentioned leads to another problem. When we look at the ethical advisor we can only test if a decision he suggests turns out right afterwards. This leads to the induction problem and one can never be sure if the next decision the advisor gives is a right one

Thirdly the way the system would be accepted by the people. This depends on how people look at the system and is for the major part a matter of trust. History has shown that people can get attached to computers and treat them like animals (such as a Japanese robot dog and a Tamagotchi pet) that people treat like it's a real pet and are completely attached to. As many ICT solutions this advisor will need to be marketed well and introduced slowly so people can slowly can used it and have some transition time. After that the advisor should only be used for advice at first and prove its value to the audience. When the advisor turns out to be useful, the audience will gradually increase its trust in the machine and accepted will be only a matter of time then. But after that, even after the system gets accepted, it will now and then disappoint people because they can't believe they are doing something wrong.

Finally the theoretical perspective. First of all the system will be part of the whole universe while it will decide about the whole universe. The sounds paradoxical and possibly it is. For example, let's say that a decision of the system leads to a bad situation, then the system has to choose different. But since the system is part of the universe, this will lead to a different thing the system has to decide about again. This could go on forever, never leading to a good decision at all. This is emphasized in theory on slides of Luciano Floridi for Microsoft Research Laboratory - PhD students summer school 2006. He talks about agents and patients and deals interactions being part of the info sphere. In addition to this I'd like to add that when the moral sense of the ethical advisor becomes so strong he might get reasons to lie about what's going in order to do better for the world. In some way this doesn't seem fair and again very paradoxical results might be expected.
