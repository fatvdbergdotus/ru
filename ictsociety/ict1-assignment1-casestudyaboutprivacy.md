## Assignment 2: Case study about privacy

Since the digital era has just arrived and has caused many implications regarding privacy, the current laws of our society aren't completely adapted to this new computerised situation. To illustrate some problems I will do a case study about blogging during work time listed on a website about internet privacy issues[4]. First I will sketch the situation and the problems that arise from it, after which I will review some aspects of two papers about privacy and apply them to this case study's situation. After that I will review the current laws on this issue and finally I will conclude with listing some things that can be done to improve the situation.

About blogging during work time. Blogging is the act of expressing your thoughts at the internet and put them at a place (could be either restricted or unrestricted) where a certain amount of people can read them depending on how easily they could be found. Investigating the time and locations (IP address trace) of the messages being posted reveals that more and more of these posts are done during working hours. This brings up a problem, because the employer is paying the employee to show productivity. To avoid this behaviour different measurements can be taken: First the employer can completely block access to the corresponding websites making it impossible at all for employees to blog, inducing no access at all, even during a break. And second the employer can log the employees on line behaviour and easily detect abuse of such manner. A combination of both measurements is possible too, restricting access in some way but also log to be sure the employee hasn't found a new way to abuse. A first reaction after seeing this measurements might be: The employee pays the employer to work, so anything that's possible to make the employee not refrain from working should be done. But as we will see later this attitude has many implications for privacy and moral issues. 
A second problem that might occur because of the blogging, is the employee expressing valuable company information on his blog. Four examples are: The employee might talk about his work and tell in some way how the work is done, which can be used by the competing company reading the blog.  Second the employee might express something illegal that's happening in the company which can lead to legal steps from the local or national authorities. Third the employee might publish information about co workers on the internet without their permission. and finally the employee might talk bad about the company or whole branch in some sort of way, resulting in a loss of costumers. Also this problem can be solved by logging and restricting access, but this has even deeper implications as shown later. 
Both discussions happen at a civil right and crime right level. The employee wants its employer to behave in a certain way which is often stated in the working contract (civil right) but also the government has to make sure that the employee's actions don't put the society at stake (crime right).

After reading the case in the previous paragraph, one might think why is this problem typically ICT and why can't the laws we've always cherished be used to this case. In other words, what makes this case unique to situation of about let's say 40 years ago. An explanation could be: 40 years ago there was telephone and many employees had telephone access their work, so they could achieve everything that's been just sketched about blogging. This is clearly not the case because of the following reasons: First telephone access is costly while using the internet is normally free or extremely cheap these days. Second a post at a blog website can reach thousands or even millions of people at the same time, while a phone call is limited to one person. Third A blog can reach people that are anonymous, while with a phone you'll have to dial a specific number. And fourth Internet activity can be logged efficiently with automated access regulation while in case of recording phone  conversations and checking them, a labour consuming job would need to be done.

But let's not bother with the phone at all. We're still dealing with humans here and all the laws we have are constructed for humans. So why would the be any difference at all? First of all the way millions of people can be reached at once. This includes people from anywhere in the world of any race, religion, gender, political preference and whatsoever. When a big group of people knows a certain fact (at once) this might have big implications for society and even cause danger. It's very important to prevent a negative thing of this kind from happening. Second the sort of anonymity (appearing anonymity) people experience, which makes them much more expressive then just being in a face to face situation. This causes people to reveal much more then desirable and has impact on the availability of the information. Third once something is posted on the internet, it might last there forever (people copy it, search engines spider the information or simply the website keeps the information forever) even when the person who has posted it reconsiders his actions and wants it removed. And finally the ease with which information is spread. All it takes is typing some on a computer and press the okay button to post. This implies a bigger chance of impulsive information spreading (such as people posting in an angry mood). Once the need for a different approach then just using our current laws becomes clear,  there's much discussion to do, which will be done in the next paragraphs.

In this paragraph I will link to a paper of  Aaron Ben-Ze’ev[2]. He talks about the way cyberspace has affected us, but first there's the right of privacy in the blogging case. The are two things concerning in the blogging case, namely: What can an employer get to know about an employee by logging him and what can the employee write to his blog? In case the employer doesn't want to log at all and also doesn't want the employee to have access to blogging resources at all, complete restriction is the only option. But if the restriction isn't possible (due lack of software resources, the employee always finding a way to avoid restriction or the employee simply needing the internet) an 
implementation of logging method is an option. The problem with the logging is, that basically every bit of internet activity the employee compasses needs to be stored. This might result into the employer finding out very delicate details about his employee (such as porn website visits) for which the logging system wasn't meant in the first case. And since the employee and employer are normally not very close, but also not strangers to each other, the need for privacy is highest according to Aaron Ben-Ze’ev. The a similar kind of incident happens when an employee writes something to a blog. When he writes to the blog during work time, it makes sense to conclude that he'll write about something just experienced (at least something that happened after his last post) which is likely to be about work. But the employee has with most of his co workers not a private but also not a stranger (he normally sees them every day) relationship as well. This means that all the information spread on the blog has a high delicacy rate. When no actual names are listed and when the blog is only meant to be read by strangers, it's not a problem. But often people introduce their blog to people around them, and even when there no names are listed then, the context could be guessed, resulting in information becoming public that might damage people in some way. This concludes us with mentioning the two key figures according to the paper's view, namely the employer and the employee. The employer has to guarantee that logging is only done when there's a realistic suspicion of abuse and that any of the gained results from logging won't be abused. While on the other hand the employee has to guarantee that everything he writes on his blog won't hurt the company and the people working for it. 


This brings us to a second paper of Richard Volkman[1]. He states the every issue that's being held about privacy, is actually about much more common rights. The rights he addresses are life, liberty and property. When this definition of privacy is actually the case, it should be possible to apply them on the blogging case and address any abuse to at least one of the three just mentioned natural rights. These natural rights are above politics, so there will be no link to the actual legal rights for the case. First the employer checking his employee. He should respect that the rights of life, liberty and property apply to his employee. This means that whatever his employee does is his freedom (liberty and indirectly the right to have a biography) and anything he writes on the blog is his property. This makes it seem that no logging is allowed at all, but there's one vital point. The employer and employee have set up a contract when the employee started working there, which states that the employee will receive a periodical wage, but also states that the employee will show productivity to benefit the employer. It's not always possible to exactly measure this level of productivity (sometimes the final product doesn't completely reflect the hours spend on it), but there might be a suspicion from the employer that the employee isn't completely devoted to his job. When this suspicion is reasonable the employer needs to find out what's going on, because the employee isn't respecting the right of the employer on the other hand. The employer has a right of property, but when there's nothing in return for his investment (salary) the employee is basically stealing. It's clear to see that the three natural laws deal with the privacy issue.
The other part of the case is about the employee sharing information on his blog about the company in some way. When the people of the company are negatively affected by those messages, their rights of life and liberty are taken away. In other words, they can't be the person they want to be and have a biography, because sensitive information about them is made public, affecting their lives in an unwantedly way. Also when the company as an institute is being disadvantaged, some natural rights are being disrespected. First of all the company is owned by shareholders or other people. Their property is being damaged, violating the right of property. Second the company itself can be seen as something having the same rights as humans, resulting in the right of life (company might go broke and/or loose viability) or right of liberty (company can't have its own policy any more) not being maintained.

In order to view the legal aspects of the case, it's needed to look at the current laws and morales on it. According to the second paper legal rights are made up by the mentioned natural rights and social circumstances. Four issues can be addressed in the case to which the law applies[3], namely: Is an employee allowed to blog at work? Can an employee blog about his work? Can the employer put restrictions on the employee's freedom of speech? Can the employee reveal illegal practices of the employee's company? These four questions can be answered from a legal perspective. The first question about an employee being able to blog at work has a two fold answer, namely the employer could forbid the use of internet for private use for the employee, but is not allowed to log all the traffic in order to prove so. This is so because it would but too much pressure on the employee risking a health hazard (for example due emotional stress). This induces that only a certain amount of suspicion justifies logging. The employee is allowed to blog about his work (second question), but needs to keep in mind that sensitive or company secret information can be revealed. Especially in companies with stocks this is important to know. For example even when an employee tells on a blog that he has a new function within the company, the impact of this news on the could be big because of it. Third the restrictions an employer might want to put on the employee's freedom of speech. Principally an employer is not allowed to put any restrictions on it, but when the information is company related he is, because it might damage the company. Finally the expressing illegal practices of the employee part, also known as whistleblowing. The employee has to realize that when he does reveal something, there will be much pressure on his relationship with the employer. Currently there are no laws on this issue which makes people's morales decisive.


This concludes with a proposal for improvement for the previously mentioned problems. The improvements can be done on four areas: Having a third party logging, changing some laws, having a decent sufficiently covering working contract and on the area of more discussion between the employee and employer. When a third party is being involved to log the company's internet traffic, this party can be completely not connected in any way to employer and employee. According to  Aaron Ben-Ze’ev's paper, the need for privacy is very low then and even when the third party finds out very delicate details that are not related to the reason why it's logging, it doesn't need to share them with the employer. This way only details that are relevant to the employer and not the embarrassing ones will be shared. Also the change of some laws is desirable. As stated in the previous chapter, laws aren't always very clear and complete. On order to achieve improvement on this, people who are experts in security can be consulted by politicians to get an insight about what still needs to be done. Third a lot of misunderstandings can be prevented by having a clear working contract. The contract can list what the employer can and cannot do and also the same for the employee. This contract can include some CAO (common working agreements) aspects, so that the restrictions the employer makes are tested to be reasonable. Finally it's important that employee and employer talk much about this topic, so that they can know what to expect from each other and see things from each other's perspective. This paragraph makes clear that one can't expect a solution that always works, but can only count on some guidelines to deal with a specific situation. This makes sense since we're dealing with humans.
