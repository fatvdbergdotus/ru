### Introduction
In the last two decades more and more information around us has made the transformation from an analog source to a digital one. VCR tapes have become DVDs, music tapes turned into CDs and our photos are mostly made by a digital camera nowadays. A major concern about this digital storage of information is the ease with which it can be copied and spread around on the internet, which is mostly a concern when the content is very delicate for a certain person or when copyrights are being infringed. Estimates are that more then 10 billion euros are lost in the year 2001 due to worldwide piracy in software and music, of which roughly 65% is contributed by software in this amount. (1)  In this paper I will show different aspects of how piracy is being dealt with current days and conclude with my own vision on this topic about how to deal with privacy in the future. The ways of dealing with privacy are two fold. On one hand there's the protection and marking of digital media with encryption, watermarking and other protection mechanisms, while on the other hand one can try to reduce piracy by dealing with (potential) violators by profiling the current violators, moralizing people and making the right laws. The former category focuses mainly on prevention and ICT possibilites while the latter one is more error controlled and focused on society.

### Watermarking and protecting digital media
Let's consider the watermarking and protection of digital media. The watermarking techniques attempt to make it possible to trace the rightful owner of digital content, while protection of digital media has the itention to make the media only available to people who are authorized in a certain way. This could be done by either copy protection or authorization before using the media.

Watermarking has been discussed by Lintin Qiao and Klara Nahrstedt(2). Two requirements concerning watermarking have been addressed, namely: The watermarking should be non invertible and costumers should be able to create their own evidence and prove their legitimate rights to use the media being watermarked. The non invertible aspect is done by manipulating some bits of the digital content which can't be undone. It's hard to imagine that this is possible, but when the watermark is spread around the whole digital media it's impossible to get rid of it without damaging the digital product. The topic of creating a feasible watermark system is highly related to security and the above paper shows prove about this being possible in mathematical terms. The second aspect, about costumers proving to be the owner, is often underestimated. When this is being ignored, one implicitly makes the assumption that the the owner of the the content (in contrast to the consumer) is trustworthy, which of course doesn't need to be the case. The basic idea behind this process is to add a mechanism to the previously already embedded watermark, to uniquely identify the costumer of the media. This way illegally copied media could easily be traced back. There are two ways to achieve this. Are third trusted party could be used to take care of the whole creation of the watermark or a protocol could be designed in which the costumers send his unique id to the owner after which the owner makes sure it's embedded in the watermark without it being used twice. Applying both mentioned techniques at one digital piece of media, results into making sure who's the owner of it and also who has the right to own it. However, the costumer can still share the media with everyone and make as many copies as he/she wishes.


In order to deal with limiting people on infringing copyrights by copying media illegally, some sort of protection mechanism needs to be added, which is the intention of copy protection. Generally the just mentioned watermarking plays a big role in copy protection but in general three systems are needed to safely ensure it: an encryption system that makes the content of the disc unreadable without knowing the key to use, a watermark to check whether the content can be played on the current device and a system for creating secure authorized copies (3). A SDMI based rights management system is being discussed by Kwok et al (4). Their concept includes a server/client architecture in which the the whole system consists of 3 parts: a rights management server, a rights management database and multimedia player. The rights management server watermarks media requested by the multimedia player, but only does this after the information of the media player has been validated by the rights management database system. This database system contains all the media and ownership. Although this system can be made to be very safe, some problems arise. First of all, the consumer can just play the media on his media player and record the sound being played with another recording device. And although some quality might get lost, this will be something that can never be prevented. Besides that the media player is often an embedded device, but when there's software running on it, the software could be manipulated, resulting in cracks or fixes to play digital content. This problem can be solved by adding a unique number in the hardware of the device. Finally the most constraining aspect: In order to do these checks and stream the media a constant network connection is needed. There have been solutions without having this last constraint, but generally they have resulted into being cracked (such as DVD encryption).

This concludes this paragraph with saying that watermarking and copy protection can be useful tools to fight privacy and to increase the effort needed to be made to use illegal copies, but on contrary to that it's important to notice that generally one can assume that when the costumer's ease needs to be maintained, it's likely that a protection technique being convenient will not hold. That's the reason why the next paragraph focuses on dealing with piracy from the society's perspective instead of just concerning the digital one.

### Fighting piracy from the society's perspective
Now that it's clear the piracy can't be fought using watermarking and other digital protection measurements only, different options need to be considered, with many focused on changing people's behavior. The different options being considered are: using the law to fight piracy, profiling abusers, teaching piracy ethics and bandwidth limiting.

First the law about piracy (5). There are different ways in which the law provides protection for intellectual property, namely: trade secret, patent, copyright and licensing. In case of a trade secret generally a formula, pattern, device or information needs to stay within the company to make sure competition doesn't get to know it and benefit from it. An employee could be leaking to the competition for money for example. Often to protect this aspect, license agreements are used with software which state that the buyer can't copy or disclose it to any third party. Next the patent law, which protects a certain idea and not the specific form, way or item it appears in. Generally patents are not appealing in software industry, because they require much money and bureaucracy to obtain and maintain. A copyright on the contrary doesn't require such consuming efforts, but generally applies to a single kind product. Some examples are: a DVD movie, a audio CD and a book. Copyrights can generally last for decades and even still remain for many years after the rightful owner has died. Finally the licensing. In most cases when a consumer buys a software product, a license agreement appears during the installation of the software and also on DVDs a message appears first while playing it. This agreement can been seen as some sort of contract that the consumer needs to agree on in order to use the digital product. The content of the agreement can be very various and might include patent and copyright information. In all cases just mentioned, infringement may lead to heavy penalties by law, including prison. The problem however is that the copyrights are often hard to check and only likely to be enforced on mass scale abuse.
Second the profiling of users is being investigated. Two papers are being used to localize this issue in which one focuses on students having certain properties likely being digital pirates (6) and the other one on determinants of copyright abusers in general (7). The paper of Sims et al. about the former topic, starts with addressing some general information about piracy, after which the explanation about specifically using the student population for this research follows. The paper states that the software piracy for people starts at a very young age, mostly when they attend college and research has shown that people who have been in the workforce longer are more ethical about this issue. In contrary to that no significant difference have been detected between undergraduate and graduate students. In addition to that the teacher's behavior as an example has been shown to contribute much in the student's behavior, and teachers are more unethical then people working at the business floor. All these aspects taken together lead to the strong impression that colleges and universities are a breading ground for digital piracy in any kind of way. Causes for this phenomenon are three fold: Students are not told what is and isn't expected from them with regard to software and hardware use, are not acquainted with the laws concerning software piracy and finally not even confronted with ethical issues in general at all. The paper concludes with an empirical experiment which shows a significant difference in gender (males committing more piracy then females), older students committing more piracy then younger ones and finally people using computers more pirating more. In addition to this the second paper of Van Kranenburg focuses on general determinants of piracy violators at macro level. In order to make it examinable economical, legal and social factors are being observed. It turns out that geographic location is a strong indicator for piracy, since the 3 factors just named tend to be relatively the same within a specific country, but differ much with other countries than that one. The conclusions of above finding can be used in two ways: First of all they could be used to detect piracy and have legal consequences of appropriate level. And second the knowledge can be used for prevention. Some examples include: adding an ethics class to universities, negotiate with countries infringing the piracy laws and supporting lower income levels with a need for specific software.

The prevention of piracy is a topic that requires some more attention. Ethics play a major role in this and are discussed in a paper by Kini et al (8). The paper focuses on different morales in the United States and Thailand. The paper describes empirical investigation leading to several conclusions: The general morales of people are highly correlated to their morales on privacy. Generally US students showed a much higher moral intensity regarding piracy then people from Thailand, which might be an indicator for the higher piracy rates in Thailand. And as stated before also the gender turns out to play a role, with men being more violating. From these conclusions some implications could be derived which are needed to change the moral attitude. First of all teachers play an important role in this process, because it turns out that students are highly influenced by fellow students on this topic. A way to achieve a positive flow between the students would be to introduce exercises that include ethical and moral principles and have students learn them from each other. Besides that university employees need to be trained well about this issue, since students come in contact with them and learn and copy their behavior. For long term improvement expectations from the researchers arise. They need to gain better understanding about the factors that cause a difference between the different cultures. Find out the aspects that make moral intensity and software piracy relate. Replicate this study to have more accurate results and double check this conclusions and finally develop study approaches that increase the moral intensity of students.


Finally some measurements that take into account that there will always be some amount of piracy but try to make the best of it. Some examples include: putting extra money on empty CDs and DVDs to win some money back lost by piracy, making a cheaper version of a software product available (eg. Student edition) to tempt users to buy the original version and finally bandwidth limiting. The last item has been investigated by Clark and Tsiaparas(9). The main point being made is that users who download much should be charged for it. One can think of different levels of access speed of the internet for different prices. The money becoming available from this could be used for the battle against piracy or to abate companies that have been disadvantaged by piracy. Another thing that could be considered is to limit the bandwidth at all from unknown sources, assuming that no person would need that kind of speed for legal activities. This is very rigorous and is probably not considered wanted from anyone's perspective. ISPs that cut bandwidth now deal with competitors that don't and hence get more costumers for offering higher speeds. In order to have all ISPs implement such rules, general laws need to be considered.

### Conclusion
In this paper the battle against piracy has been the central theme. Two different categories have been defined of which one deals with piracy from the ICT perspective and the other from the society's perspective. The ICT solutions watermarking and copyright protection have been proposed, but have been concluded to be not sufficient solely. Society opportunities were categorized in laws, piracy profiling, teaching morales and accepting there's piracy but keeping it under control measurements. All measurements used on a single basis turned out to have their imperfections and in my opinion in order to fight piracy a combination of measurements is a must. In this paper I've been trying to give an overview about what's been considered so far and what still needs to be done to get piracy under control. Some steps that need to be taken are: better quality copyright protection,  more attention for piracy in the law, mapping why people steal digital content, changing the general morales about illegally copying digital content and taking measurements that discourage piracy, such as making empty CDs and DVDs more expensive and limiting the bandwidth at ISPs, resulting in longer download times. In addition to that research need to be done on the psychological aspect of piracy and although piracy has been ignored and underestimated for a long time, the number of measurements that can be taken are very diverse. This concludes me with saying there's still hope in the battle against piracy.
