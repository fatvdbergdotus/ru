## Overview
As can be guessed from the introduction, this report will take care of the effects ICTs have on culture integration. What is meant by culture integration is that people move to a foreign country and have to find their way there. Often the language is different, but this is just a matter of learning. A more challenging topic with regard to the integration is the way people deal with a different culture, which often can't be described in words and rules. To see the effects the ICTs have on this topic, a comparison between before and after the ICTs arrived needs to be made.

With respect to the integration and ICTs four different subcategories can be defined, which are a generalisation of everything mentioned in papers dealing with this topic. First one can look at how much people try to keep their own culture. This can be determined by how much people keep their old countries' habits, speak their native language and stay in touch with their country of home. From the integration's perspective it's wanted that these aspects are limited as much as possible. Another way the staying in touch with culture is expressed is by determining at what level the migrant stays in touch with people of the same country as theirs in the new country compared to the contacts they have with native people there. Often this is relatively easily possible, because multicultural countries tend to have big groups of ethnic minorities.

Second there's the efforts that are being made to understand the new culture. Some indicators for this are the level at which the person can speak the language of their new country (strongly correlated with the language spoken at home), the amount of communication being done with people from the new country and the involvement in activities in the new country.

Third there's the way in people feel involved in being part of the culture of the new country. This is one step further then just understanding the culture. When people reach this level, they would like to change something about the new culture they don't like and are willing to make the new country a better place, not just for themselves but also for the native people living there. In addition to that the people are really willing to accept that native people might have different values then them and even though they might not agree on them, still tolerate them.


Finally there's the way the ethnic minorities are being judged by the native people. This might not be completely in control of the minorities, but plays a major role in the way opportunities are being offered to be successful in the new country. Bad indicators on this topic include the amount of racism and discrimination towards the ethnic minority, such as job opportunities being offered and abilities being offered for the minorities to express themselves freely. A good indicator would be the case in which the person being from a different native country isn't even noticed by the native people.

The four different categories just listed provide a categorization in which different topics mentioned in various articles about the topic could be placed. For every category it's possible to compare the situation before and after ICTs are being introduced and list the good and bad aspects ICTs have had on them.

## Consequences
First every paper will be investigated in the same order as just mentioned. This will provide an overview of the different articles with respect to the framework and having the relevant contents of the articles linked to it. 

First the article of Lenie Brouwer. It's starts with saying that the internet offers Muslim migrants new ways of communication. Websites are being set up and to increase the interaction chat rooms, forums and mailing lists are often part of them. Due to the way the internet is being organized these websites are often even transnational. Compared to the traditional media such as books and possibly television, the new media offer a down-market quality, because production is cheap and easily done. On the other side by expanding the public domain, more people are being reached and able to take part in the communication. The article continues with introducing a mailing list, then shows different general topics being discussed on it and finally links what has happened to the literature. The general topics that caused a lot of activity on the list are 'are Muslim women allowed to use make up in public?' (1999), 'The Dutch Stereotypes' (January 200) and 'The consequences of 9/11' (September 2001 and later). The first topic deals with the Muslim believe that a woman shouldn't do anything to tempt a man at all. A veil is a good example in which the habit is expressed, since the coverage of the hair of the woman is supposed to take away temptation from the men observing her.  Discussion found place because in western societies such restrictions for a woman don't take place and Muslim women started to question why they are forced to behave a certain way. Another post of the discussion actually stated the opposite, but telling that in western societies women have to bring their children to day care so that they can work and that in addition to that they're expected to do housekeeping and be a good wife for their husband in several ways. When the above is linked to the framework point two and three are mainly addressed. Muslim people get aware about what's going on in the western culture and besides that even question whether they deserve changes to adapt to that culture more, simply because there are things in the new culture that might be appealing to them. The second topic about the Dutch stereotypes is mostly referring to the way Muslim people are seen by the Dutch society. In the framework this links to number four. The treat makes Muslim people aware about the way they are often seen and presented in Dutch society, which might help them in acting in such a way that these prejudices can be proven wrong. Finally there's the consequences of the terrorist attacks in the USA that are being discussed. People get to share their thoughts and pain about the way Muslims were being seen after the strikes. Also this contributes to point four and sharing those thoughts might lead to cooperation to show the Dutch society Muslims are mostly different then the people that did the attacks.


Second there's the paper of Bharat Mehra et al. The paper is not completely useful for the context and thus specific parts of it will be used. One paragraph is about how internet research can empower African-American women. Because of the digital divide the differences between poor and rich have become bigger, but putting effort in supporting minorities which are in this case minorities by race, a lot of new chances could be offered to them relating to their spirtual, physical, emotional and intellectual health. Two examples given include a request for information about diet, keeping fit and  other health related issues. Internet and digital communication can play a very positive role in this area. Matching this to the framework getting the way around in the society plays the most important role for offering opportunities to the people. Another paragraph deals with how to get those minorities to use the internet, without considering its usefulness. It shows that the digital divide is clearly present and that even though there might be good results on the four parts of the framework, there will be still always a group that might not benefit from what the ICTs have to offer. This aspect will be taken in consideration for the general advantages ICTs have to offer.

Third the article of Leen D'Haenens being published in 2003. The article starts with explaining that the internet usage globally is increasing significantly and that the number of foreign people in The Netherlands is rising and rise. From those two statements consequences could be derived which will be discussed. Another point being mentioned is that the ethnic minorities normally have less access to the internet then the native people, mostly because of the costs being involved with it. This is illustrated by 36,5% of the blacks and 35,6% of the Latino's in the USA being online at least 15 hours a week, which is in deep contrast with the 54,6% in the whole country. This is comparable to the digital divide being introduced when discussing the previous paper. This is also translated to the Dutch situation in which 30% of the Moroccans and 20% of the Turks don't even know what internet is at all. A way to deal with this is to introduce the internet and digital interaction in the schools. Because of this the gap isn't so big in the younger generation and the higher education one, implying that the digital divide shouldn't be a problem in the far future. A big overview with numbers about ICT usage, having internet access and the way different kind of ethnic groups perform on them support these findings in the continuation of the article. Next a more interesting point is being struck. The reason why certain findings are done is being mentioned. It starts with stating that the very religious Turkish youth uses the internet much more then the ones not being involved or less in a faith. Media are used to satisfy certain needs, an idea which can be passed on to ICTs as well. This implies that religion causes some sort of need. 
An overview of different kind of people being in a foreign country is being given with respect to media. Homelanders don't pay any attention to the new country's media while gradually the omnivores spend some attention to it and the adapters focus completely on the new country's media. This effects the first two parts of the framework in which the homelanders mostly are positive in the first part about trying to keep their own culture and the adapters mostly in the second part in paying efforts to understand the new culture. This dimension is highly correlated to what norms and values are being respected, with the adapters mostly focusing on the new country's norms and values. One can question if ICTs have an effect on people ending up in a certain category and when it doesn't if ICTs still effect the way people behave in a certain category. What is mentioned right away is that in the particular case of the Dutch government, a focus on traditional media is being made such as television, radio and newspapers. This lead to different initiatives to make the media more coloured, but this approach underestimates the effect what ICTs can have. As a consequences of this the paper tells that this lacking lead to 'foreign' people turning to their homeland media. This illustrates that in order to have ethnic minorities watch the media of their new country and to get used to hearing its values and norms, ICTs are a very essential need. A recent shift in government policy in the Netherlands already paid off in citizens being more involved, including the minority people.


Finally the second paper by Leen D'Haenens. First of all ICTs have become cheaper and much more user friendly, which resulted in a use my the majority of the people. However, as a result of the digital divide, some catching up still needs to be done by certain groups of people in society. ICTs provide two functions, the ability to resolve cultural differences and the ability to support the culture and identity of an ethnic cultural group. In the framework these functions can be addressed in part one about keeping the original culture and part two about getting to know the new culture (part three might play a role whenever the understanding of the new culture results into getting involved in it above that). When understanding is being made during resolving differences, part 4 about how ethnic minorities are being judged is supported as well. The former function is based on the two-way communication between a specific group and society, while on the other hand the latter copes with communication within a given group. The paper presents earlier surveys being done in America in the end 1980's with African, Latino and white youngsters after which a survey is being done in Netherlands and Belgium. The survey deals with six groups of people, namely Flemish Turks, Dutch Turks, Flemish Moroccans, Dutch Moroccans, native Flemish and native Dutch. For every group a sample of the people has been picked, varying from 98 people till 287 people. Different properties have been asked and tested on them. These include: Language fluency (better Dutch/better homeland languague, equally good),  Religious outlook (very/not at all), Ethnocultural position (low/high), PC access, Internet connection, location of computer usage, reason for computer usage. When all this data is being combined and statistically analysed, correlations could be detected. Native people have more access to ICTs then ethnic minorities is the first conclusion and when the minority youth has a computer is normally in their bedroom instead of being accessible to the whole family. Also ethnic minorities tend to use the computer for more well-target purposes instead of just entertainment, such as seeking information on religion and culture and participating in newsgroups of such a manner. Something very relevant to the topic of the paper is that the survey and research have shown that ICT usage among young ethnic minorities lead to bridging. This is expressed by more then 1 out of 3 people keeping in touch with native people through email. Moroccans have expressed feeling involved more with Dutch society because of this (point 3 of the framework). On the other hand, which can be placed in point one of the framework about keeping the original culture, is that email is also used for staying in touch with the original country. 


## My opinion
In this paper I've been providing a framework as an anchor for the literature about ICTs affecting integration. For every part of this framework ICTs have a big effect and this effect can be negative as well positive. Based on the literature and the conclusions derived from it, I will reflect my own opinion on every topic with a general conclusion for each topic.

First in the framework, there's the way ethnic minority people are still trying to keep their own culture. Due email and internet communication people can stay in touch with their homeland and hear what's going on there. A negative side effect from this might be the point that people are not being forced to let go of their own culture and might spend all their time on living in the world of their own culture. However, research has shown that staying in touch with the original culture doesn't have this effect and mostly can just have a comforting effect and making the step to move to another country not so big at once. This results into people being more happy and able to talk with their homeland about things they experience at ask for advice from that perspective.


Second there's the efforts people make to understand the new culture they ended up in. The negative effect of ICTs could be once again that people of ethnic minority spend all their time on talking with people from their homeland of even with ethnic people of the same kind in their new country. But actually the second part has proven to show some great benefits. Whenever some major event happens in a country that effect the minorities there, the internet is being used massively to express feelings about it and share thoughts about it. This happens between the minorities, but also many times native people of the country are involved in this, leading to a better understanding about what's going on and what the different parties are thinking. In addition to that there's communication of the ethnic minorities living in one country. Most Moroccan and Turkish people in the Netherlands for example are Muslim. When something happens in The Netherlands that they can't understand well, they start to discuss about it with the help of ICTs. The more people get involved in these discussions, the more clear becomes what's going on and even when the minorities don't agree with it, they'll still have the possibility to advice each other about how to deal with what's going on. 

Third there's the being involved in the new culture part. Above the just mentioned part, it requires besides understanding to even participate in the new culture. Due ICTs big groups of people can start some sort of front, in which they can share their voice and truly make a difference. A good example of this is the weblog of Jan Marijnissen of the Dutch SP party, in which he reviews his vision on a news article periodically. People get to reply to it and discuss about it and by looking at what's going on there, the SP party can draw it's conclusions. Other ways in which this part is positively highlighted is the use of E-democracy, E-culture (sharing music and other forms of art and entertainment) and E-communication.

Finally there's the way minorities are being judged by native people. Often racism and other kinds of hate evolve from misunderstanding. ICTs have made communication easy and cheap. Different forum websites are about topics that are in people's interest and are very lively in society, including cultural issues. Native people get involved in these discussions too, both on native websites as well on minority websites.  

On contrast to these mainly positive effects of the ICTs there are some negative things to that need to be taken into account. This leads to a risk analysis and a plan of aspects that need to be dealt with for the future. 

As mentioned before there's digital divide in the world, not just geographically, but even within countries. Native people in a country generally seem to have more access to ICT resources. When this doesn't change the benefits of ICTs will near reach the minorities as much as hoped for, but the effect might be even worse. When the digital divide remains the misunderstanding and jealousy between native and ethnic minority people might even increase, resulting in more and more conflicts and a very unbalanced power position.


What also needs to be considered is the attitude governments have towards the new media. Often governments seem to support the old media a lot and make sure that everything what's happening there is coloured, but underestimate and don't understand well that younger people, which are the future, can't be reached that way. This underestimation leads to the effect of ICTs being diminished and ethnic minority people turning towards their native media, without being informed about their new country.

To conclude this, the risks of radicalism in religion with respect to ICTs shouldn't be underestimated. Due it's easy way of spreading information and reaching a lot of people, it's possible to share very negative thoughts quickly and efficiently and to provide a platform in which everything of this matter can be learned. A way to deal with this is to have decent laws on this topic and more important to have them enforced in a strong way by blocking websites and prosecuting website owners providing such content.

After the papers have been shortly introduced and their relevance has been explained, the contents of the papers have been discussed and at the same time being linked to the framework. This way the different parts of the framework are filled with pros and cons.

The paragraph after that deals with me expressing my opinion of ICTs on the different aspects of the framework, based on what just has been discussed about the papers. Besides some minor disadvantages the introduction of ICTs has a positive effect on every aspect. However, as the paragraph about my opinion continues, three future risks are being labelled that need some attention to have a better future on the integration by ICT topic. First of all there's the digital divide, which means ethnic minority people might be limited in the computer and internet access. Second there's the conservative approach of the government towards the new media, which might result into the ICT not being maximally used and finally there's the treat of radical ideas spreading around on the internet and causing negative attitudes and actions. However, with the right approach these three aspects can be solved.
