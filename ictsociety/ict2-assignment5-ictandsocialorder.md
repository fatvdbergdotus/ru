## Evolution of the music industry
In order to illustrate how the music industry has evolved two papers are used, namely 'The internet is changing the music industry' [1] and 'Music Publishing to MP3' [2]. Both papers deal with giving an overview of how the music industry has changed over time, of which the former paper mostly focuses on the digital era and its consequences, while the latter paper tries to give an overview that completely starts at the beginning of music production.

Firstly the paper 'Music Publishing and MP3'. The paper starts with mentioning that the music industry has been a driven a lot by Great Britain, Western Europe and the United States due being industrialized nations. The fact that two world wars were fought in Europe, made it that the position of the United States became even more dominant. Recently however the music industry has crossed national boundaries and achieved a global character. Three phases are mentioned in which different kind of organizations were powerful in the music industry. Chronologically they are:
- Music publishing houses: At the time music was only available on sheets, people had to visit a performance to get to hear music. Also when music started to become recordable, it took a while for this to change.
- Record companies: Music was recorded and achieved dominance
- Transnational entertainment companies: These companies promote music as an ever expanding series of revenue streams. The music was independent of the carrier. This includes internet streams but also record sales. Another property of these companies is that music does not contribute to all the income they have.

In the music publishing houses era, the piano was the main piece of the house. This is illustrated by 300 000 pianos being sold annually in the USA between 1900 and 1914. At this time recording was really a sideline business. The origins of this ear were around 1450, when Gutenberg invented movable type. Literature rose and also music would follow quickly in the form of sheets. In the early 18th century the financial interests of merchant bookseller-publishers expanded resulting in the first copyright law in Britain in 1710 called the Statute of Anne. The law included an author's copyright and protection for consumers, but favored the stationer's guild thus granting power to music publishing houses. Compared to writers however, the law suppressed musical material. It was not until the end of the 18th century until it become possible to make a living from printing and selling music. At the same time the market for pianos grew and having an instrument became a status symbol. At the 19th century the interest in copyright systems for the music industry got bigger, because music more then other arts crossed national and cultural boundaries. The first international copyright act was enacted in 1838 in Britain and extended to include music in 1842. 

After this the era in which the recording companies had the power came. In 1877 Thomas Edison unveiled his legendary talking machine, which is considered the birth of recording. His invention had poor sound at first and were therefore not considered to be used for music right from the start. In 1889 Glass placed dictating machines in the Palais Royal Saloon in San Francisco where patrons could listen to entertainment for a nickel. This made Glass the father of the Jukebox. In 1888 Emile Berliner made it possible to make multiple copies of one record using only one master. There were still technical, legal and financial boundaries though. The quality was poor, writers and publishers did not receive royalties from recorded music yet and mass production was still relatively hard. In the 1910s there were two record companies that divided their market share all over the world. In 1928 a copyright with respect to moral right was introduced, which protected an author against someone editing or altering his work that could compromise his integrity. Parallel to the developments just mentioned radio broadcasting become more and more popular as well. This resulted into commercial radio in the 1920s in America, starting in 1920 involving the Westinghouse station KDKA in Pittsburgh, USA going live with a regular broadcasting program. In the 1930s a coast to coast radio network became reality in the USA. In 1937 ASCAP made 5.9 million income out of radio. In the 1940s structural change happened through technological advances. BASF and Telefunken developed the magnetic tape in Germany. This invention made it possible to edit and copy music for anyone. Other advantages included a lower price, portability and more durable. In addition to that Bell Telephone invented the transistor in 1948, which made it possible to amplify the signal on the tape. Also the transistor lead to truly portable radio receivers. In the same year the LP at 33rpm record was invented which soon became the standard for albums in the music industry. In the 1950s rock and roll arose and contributed to 42.7 percent market share in 1959. Since Rock and Roll puts a high aesthetic value on repetition and improvisation, it favored African ways of making music over European ways. It created changes for African Americans to make music, that were not there before. The 1960s were the period of commercial expansion and corporate consolidation. 

Next the part about the transnational music companies. In the 1980s music became a more increasingly global phenomenon. The was illustrated by accepting more that non-Western artists made music, such as Youssou N'Dour. Michael Jackson's album Thriller sold over 40 million copies in 1983 and a big proportion of all the revenues were created by a dozen number of artists in the years after that. A big part of this population of artists were black, such as Lionel Richie, Janet Jackson, Whitney Houston and Tina Turner. The transnational music companies began to see themselves as exploiters of rights instead of producers of records. The invention of the CD by Philips and Sony in the early 1980s supported the distribution of music, by offering good quality for cheap production costs. However when in the late 1990s CD recording technology became affordable, people were able to copy and edit  a CD without any loss of quality. This started to happen on large scale and the introduction of MP3 which made it possible to store high quality music files on a much smaller space (about 11 times as small as before) supported this development. After this the internet become more commonly used and provided ways for people to exchange information. This is where the second paper continues.

Secondly the paper 'The internet is changing the music industry'. The paper has been written in 2001 and therefore deals with the early phase of illegal distribution of music. Compression techniques are getting better, such as MP3 and on the other hand the bandwidth is increasing rapidly. Both developments have contributed to making it possible to spread around data through the internet more and more and not needing to have physical interchange (such as a DVD or CD) anymore. This is illustrated by Napstar, an music exchanging community on the internet mostly being used to exchange illegal music, having 80 million users at its peak. As a countermeasure there has been a successful lawsuit against Napster by the RIAA, which required Napster to install a filter to filter out illegal music. This basically meant the end of Napster. Also alternatives to MP3 which enforce copyright have been considered, such as a2b Liquid Audio and Microsoft Media Technology.


What is mentioned by the paper that the big problem in this case is not MP3 for the music industry, but that there is a new distribution channel, which is dictated by consumer. Through this channel consumers can purchase individual songs instead of whole albums from whomever they please. This is done by search engines on the internet and does not require retailers anymore to make songs available to the consumer. So in addition to the first paper in which three eras are mentioned with respect to having power in the music industry, a fourth in which the consumers themselves have the power can be added. This has some consequences for the future of which companies who would like to attend in the music industry have to meet the following constraints:
- Make music available anywhere (all over the world), anytime (24/7) and anyhow (including video and at any given format)
- Make music creation your core competency.
- Make strategic alliances with Internet and media companies to develop decent standards for encoding music and to be able to be found on internet.

Everything written in this chapter can be linked back to the sub research question it is trying to answer as mentioned in the introduction: 
- How has the music industry evolved over time in terms of the medium used and whom having the power?

By using two papers it became clear that the person or companies having the power in the music industry has shifted three times. Those shifts have mainly been a consequence of the medium used to distribute the music to the consumer. Initially there were not really ways to record music. Due to Gutenberg it became possible to print sheets of music massively. This does however not imply right away that one has music one can listen to. In order to achieve that a performer needs to physically play the music to an audience. Because of this the power was in the hands of the music producing houses. After this era recording became possible and also broadcasting to the radio was an option. This causes a shift from the actual producers to the record companies with respect to power, since one could broadcast songs very often, without having to need someone to perform all the time. When copyright came more into place, music achieved a more global character  and the media on which music was spread became more sophisticated in terms of quality and production costs such as CD, the Transnational entertainment companies took over the power. Instead producing music or caring about the music itself, they became companies trading in the rights on songs but also other multimedia such as video. The third shift, which caused a forth era to occur, happened with the availability of the internet's high enough speeds to share music. In this era the consumer became in charge, because he got access to a channel of distribution which he could completely shape. 

The last era is the current situation. What can be learned from the different eras is that a new approach is needed in everyone of them to survive. Since we are only at the beginning of the forth era, the music industry does not exactly know how to deal with it and shows to be very conservative. This is something that is important to know in order to estimate the effect the internet will have on the music industry.




## Internet and distribution
As can be seen in the previous chapter, the internet has had a big impact on the way music has been distributed. Instead of listening to the radio or going to the store to buy a CD, people can know have access to resources of the same kind through the internet. This is expressed in downloading a song from the internet or listening to a radio stream on the internet. To put this change in perspective and to check whether this change can be generalized to different kind of products, this chapter will deal with a view on business on the internet in general. This is done by examining two papers which are 'The internet and the revolution in distribution'[3] and 'Business Models for Internet based E-Commerce'[4].

The first paper deals with three areas in which the distribution experienced a revolution through the internet becoming available on large scale. Besides the music industry, which is one of the categories, the retailing industry and the financial industry is considered in this paper. Each one is discussed separately after which the conclusions are generalized to managerial lessons that can be drawn from it. 

The internet has had a big impact on the channels of distribution and because of that the supply chains have changed for a lot of products. Instead of having a long supplier chain which involves a lot of parties, it is now often possible to order things on the internet directly at the factory which produces it (or only one more step away). This results in a faster delivery, less stocks and higher competition. For the people that are attending on the market, it means that they have to be more careful in everything they do, since margins have become minimal. A distinction between two kinds of companies is made, namely the pure info-mediaries and the hybrid intermediaries. The former category depends completely on its skills to provide information (for example Ebay which matches buyers and sellers without ever touching the merchandise) while the latter still have some physical distribution. A good example here is Amazon, which sells books and all kinds of other products, but also makes it easier to search through them by having some sort of catalog. In addition to this not everything Amazon sells is delivered by Amazon itself, but instead of that 3rd party vendors are used. Before the internet was there information gathering and processing was relatively slow, because it was constraint by the limits of human workers. Because of the internet different companies can connection to each other quickly, all the way through the supply chain. This has lead to web technologies being a main driver for how the distribution process is organized.

Firstly there is the view on online retailers. Although the internet has caused a lot of advantages for distribution in retail, there are still reasons to apply the traditional way of retails. These reasons are:
- A customer wants to see, touch or smell a product
- A customer wants to interact with the in-store personnel in order to ask for advice or to be helped with the purchasing process
 The disadvantages are the following:
- Generally a higher price
- More effort from the customer needed
- A smaller product variety

Because of the internet therefore an erosion of brand equity and customer loyalty and price competition occurs. In order to deal with this a business needs to follow a different strategy to survive in which there are three key points. First of all the company needs to think about what it online presence should look like. Both companies that were traditional retailers as companies that are experienced with catalogs can transform their company to an online presence. Secondly the company can use its presence on the internet to get information from its (potential) customers. Customers are often willing to spend a remarkable amount of time on customizing the serving experience. This is a great way to hear the voice of the customer. Besides that there are many tools available that capture statistics about visitors and their behavior. Thirdly retailing can be a market for imagination. This means the original store can be improved by the new data that comes available as mentioned above and also computers in the store, in which for example one can search through the catalog, can be of added value.

Secondly there is the banking, brokerage and financial services sector. Before companies in the security brokerage industry made money for trading securities for their clients, but because of the internet the customers have become do-it-yourself people, which means they do not need companies for this anymore. Instead of this the focus should be to offer services for trading on line and in addition to this focus on giving advice about what to do. The internet has caused a lot of this information to be available for free on other websites, which make money out of advertising. Firms like e-Trade and Ameritrade offer a variety of services that were unimaginable a few years till decades ago. Four business plans are suggested to deal with this market which can be categorized as follows:
- Full service brokers: brokers that offer a wide variety of services. These services include proprietary research and analysis, a professionally manager investment strategy and superior customer service. Also trading service as their core business are included in the services package.
- Discount brokers: Provide a smaller version of the above services, but have a lower price tag in return.
- Deep discount brokers: Above trading services none are hardly any other services are offered by these company. The main goal of these companies is to make the trading commission and costs as low as possible, without really caring what the consumers are trading in.
- Electronic discount brokers: These brokers offer the lowest transaction costs for trading by making virtually everything electronic and offering no additional services. A physical office does not need to exist for these companies and their presence might only be noticed on line.
In the physical world this has resulted in a reduction in the number of physical offices and in the case physical offices do exist they are often equipped with a restaurant or a place to have coffee, which does not make the focus on the financial services solely. In the Netherlands an example here is the Postbank, which has merged with a book store, instead of solely providing post and banking services.


Finally the paper deals with the music industry. The music industry's ways of work have been mentioned already a lot in the previous chapter of this paper, but over there mostly a description of the way of working has been given. Instead of that this paper deals with the business plans the companies should use. First of all the competition has increased because of the internet, because the consumer got more options of where to buy its music. This calls for a new way of rethinking the revenue streams by the music companies. A second important thing that needs to be done is the development of standards. Music can be downloaded in different formats which have their own compatibility and needs. When the number of standards becomes to big or when a company chooses a standard that is not supported enough, there will be a real change this will affect the viability of the company. The best way to prevent this is to actively join the standard group and participate in developing the standards in order to have a final product that is satisfying to both the consumer as company to work with. Thirdly it is important to estimate well how be the revolution is the internet will bring. This requires flexible business plans and being open for new ideas from the companies.

Looking at the effect of the internet in the three kinds of branches as just mentioned, some general lessons can be derived from them about how to line up the business. Since the paper has been written a few years ago and focuses on what needs to happen in the future, it mostly describes the situation that is going on now in the successful companies. The previously mentioned lessons answer the sub research question as initially proposed which is:
- How has the internet changed the way of distribution and trading?

Firstly an organization that deals with the internet needs to be dynamic and flexible. Product development cycling need to be shortened. This is done by integrating the different parts of the business cycles and product development process. This also requires detecting new market opportunities right away and once identified, being willing and able to switch to them as quickly as possible. This makes time an essential properties in internet competition.

Secondly the customer needs to be actively involved in creating products and services. The internet makes it possible to gather a lot of data about customers with relatively effort and all this data can be used to support figuring out what the customer wants. Also the internet offers room for new products that were not imaginable before and because of these extra opportunities the customer can be served better. The risk here is however, that the competitor understands better what the customer wants then the corresponding company. Investing time and money in figuring out what the customers wants is therefore more important than it has ever been.

Thirdly marketing is important. This includes developing customer relationships and promoting a successful brand. Since switching costs are really low on line, customers tend to quickly change to another firm when they are not satisfied about something. A personal approach is needed here to keep the customers and make sure they will buy products, even when another company offers a better price. Electronic surveys, listening to the customer and marketing one-to-one are essential things to do on this area. When a company manages detailed individual profiles of customers well, it will lead to loyalty of the customers, because the customer will feel appreciated and will be able to be served better. An example here is the website Amazon.com which gives an individual recommendations about what he might be interesting in, depending on his previous purchasing behavior, what similar customers prefer and the clicks and search requests he has made on the website before.

The next paper named ' Business Models for Internet based E-Commerce' also deals with business on the internet in general and aims to define a framework in which business to business and business to customer plays a role. This is achieved by proposing a three dimensional framework and applying it to the market structure. Initially the paper shows by using a literature study a categorization of the internet economy in three segments, namely:
- Portals: A community of consumers of information about products and services
- Market makers: Matching a community of consumers with a community of producers through for example a market place
- Product/service providers: Companies producing products or services which deal with their consumers directly having a narrow supply chain

It turns out that after a while this categorization is not easy to apply anymore, since more and more companies and websites turn out to be mixing different activities. This demands a new approach. In the given approach three dimensions are mentioned each having their own characteristics. The dimensions are called value streams, revenue streams and logistic streams.

Starting with the value streams it is obvious that a website should offer some sort of value to the consumer. This value can be a lower price, convenient shopping or combining different services that are often bought together. Also bringing people together that can be useful to each other (buyer and selling, shared interests) which might not have got to know each other without the internet contributes added value. The value streams make sure that customers will stay loyal or come back to the website frequently, because there is a benefit for them in doing so. Four possible value streams are identified in this category which are:
- Virtual communities: Bringing people together that have the same focus and could therefore benefit from each other
- Reduction of transaction costs: By comparing prices easily, shortening the supply chain length and increasing competition lower prices can be achieved
- Exploiters of information asymmetry: This includes search models and bargaining models which make information available in a different way then used to be possible before the internet was there. The major example is Google.com here.
- Value added market making process: Providing a secure environment in which different people can interact or do business


In contrast to the value streams that focus on the long term, there are revenue streams that deal with the short term benefit. In order for a company to survive money needs to be made in the present. Six streams are identified here:
- Increased margins: When the supply chain is shortened or when the transaction costs are reduced, the prices of a product could drop. In addition to this a fair share can go to the business which can then make extra money per product or service through the internet.
- Online seller communities: When websites provide a free membership they can get to build a community and learn a lot about the members of the community. This information can be used for marketing purposes. This includes information of individual members but also accumulated information over a group.
- Advertising: When there are visitors on a website with banners, a certain percentage will click on them and the business will make money out of it
- Variable pricing strategies: Normally when products are delivered electronically the initial product is expensive but the variable costs after that are almost zero. Especially when a mass amount of products is sold, the margins are very big in total.
- Exploiting information asymmetry: When buyers and sellers are matched, an amount of commission fee can go to the business providing such services.
- Free offerings: Initially a product is given for free, but after a while costs need to be made to have extra functionality with the product. An example here is Adobe's PDF format.

Finally the logistic streams are discussed. A main point being made here is that a business is through the internet able to position itself anywhere in the supply as wanted or needed. This can lead to the following benefits:
- Dis-intermediation: Reducing the supply chain size
- Infomediation: Address information requirements for the users and automatically look for that right information.
- Meta-mediation: Connecting different businesses with each other

The dimensions are mentioned in the paper match the concepts of the previous paper relatively well. The value streams can be compared to having to figure out what the consumer wants and focuses on the long term viability of the business. The revenue streams can be linked to marketing, because selling a product and making a profit is a short term issue here. Note that however, marketing also focuses partly on the long term by setting up a brand and creating goodwill. Finally the logistic streams make sure that the business can be flexible and is mostly information oriented. Since the mapping goes relatively well, this paper does not have much extra value to the sub research question in addition to the previous paper, but does confirm and justify what has been mentioned in the former one.






## Internet and the music industry's laws and economy 
This chapter deals with the effect the internet has on the laws and the economy of the music industry. This is done using two papers namely ' The impact of the Internet on the law and economics of the music industry'[5] which deals with the economy and laws and 
' The effect of Internet piracy on CD sales'[6] dealing with piracy.

Firstly the former paper dealing with the laws and economy. The paper starts with describing the traditional revenue streams and then describes the effect of the internet on this model. In addition to that the enforceability of the copyright law is examined, which has changed due to Napster's peer-to-peer invention. This is concluded with an analysis of salient economic, political, legal and technological issues arising from these changes on the whole music industry.

The music industry has three principal segments, which are:
- Purchases of recorded music: Sales of CDs, cassette tapes and downloadable music
- Broadcasting recorded music: Radio channels, television channels and internet streams
- Attending live performances: Concerts and festivals
Every of these segments create revenue. The streams begin with a composer and lyricist that write a song. The song writer might publish the song himself, but in other cases other parties might buy the rights. Performers then play the song, after which the above streams split up. The major difference the internet causes after this phase is that the distribution and making copies and such is virtually costless. This means however, that making illegal copies of the media also because more tempting since it basically means obtaining music for free. The important part regarding the distribution of the revenues involves the performer's portion. Initially this amount depending on the number of sold records. Since the internet is starting to play a major role in the industry it is not so clear anymore how much is sold or how much revenue is made from the music, because one deals with virtual products and might not always be able to trace exactly the benefits and revenues the music has generated. In addition to this the internet allows producers to skip some steps in the value chain, but since retailers want to keep their portion they are try hard to keep their power. And finally illegal copying and piracy as just mentioned is much more common as before.

With respect to broadcasting a shift from radio stations to internet streams has been made. Radio stations normally got their music for free as a form of promotion and just had to pay the publisher a small amount for every time they played the song. With the use of streaming media a different situation occurs, because the consumer asks for certain music on demand. The result of this is that internet radio stations also have to pay the recording a royalty in addition to the publisher. With respect to live performances not much changes occur because of the internet, but this stream is worth mentioning because it contributes to a big part of the income that is generated at all for the artist.

Secondly the political and legal issues. Three key points are mentioned with respects to them. The industry keeps on challenging digital formats that do not respect copyrights such as mp3. This caused Mp3.com having to delete mp3 files without copyrights from their servers, because the industry did not give Mp3.com permission to do so. It is however still possible to convert a legally bought disc to an mp3 file and put it on your mp3 player.


In addition to the previous the industry extended their copyright protections. This is done by adding some mechanism into sound files that make them play only on a certain computer. Besides this laws are made that royalties have to be paid for every mp3 that is send over the internet which holds a copyright.

Finally through music music rights the 1998 Millenium Copyright Act has been enacted, which states that the websites can have access to the catalogs of the major music companies with respect to the songs they hold. So far these companies have been to slow with providing this information. This act should eventually benefit the customer in two ways: The music should become available quicker and is more findable and competition is stimulated which leads to a lower price.

Economically the internet has had an impact as well of which the following are the major characteristics:
- Increased freedom for producers and consumers to design the product: For consumers this means that the product can be accessed in different ways and for the producers that new way of billing are possible
- Mergers that involve the creation and distribution of music
- Early prototype searching for sustainable business models

Technologically the industry is looking for software and hardware solution that respect the copyright laws. This includes moving away from the traditional mp3 format. In respond to that many efforts are made to override these protection mechanisms.

The paper 'The effect of Internet piracy on CD sales' focuses on the new possibilities the internet has brought with respect to illegally or unwantedly distributing music. The effect of illegal downloads with respect to the CD sales is taken into account here and this includes seeing whether the claim music industry makes about potential loss of profit can be justified. In order to access whether this is the case, an approach involving two steps is taken which are:
- Using data on music downloads gathered in 16 countries for the period 2000-2001 and aim to find a significant effect of mp3 downloads on CD sales.
- Asses the plausibility of the estimates by analyzing survey data on mp3 downloads in the USA.

Besides the piracy there are different factors that could effect the sale of CDs. Some major one of them are the price, the national income, new distribution channels and the quality of the music. The paper defends why either they do not play a role or compensates from them in the data. The data is taken from IFPI World reports and reports the year 2000 and 2001. Taken all the possible variables in account that might have an impact and expressing them in a formal way, leads to a formula in which the number of CDs sold depends on all of them (eg. having a higher national income will lead to more CDs being sold). The effect the internet piracy has according to the data and the given interpretation is generally around 5%. This needs however to be verified using an inquiry.


The base for this inquiry includes five hypothesis which are:
- User who download music illegally, share it with others which is referred to as a multiplier
- Only regular downloading people are a threat to copyright owners
- These users reduce their net demands by one to five CDs on average
- Work internet users, broadband internet users and students have the same download behavior
- Home internet users have the same music downloading behavior regardless of whether they only access from work or also from work.
When these hypotheses are assumed to be true, it turns out that piracy accounts for selling three CDs less per person in the population. The consumption per capita was 4.3 units in the USA. From this one can conclude that piracy replaces 70% of the original CD sales. This is however an upper bound, because the people that download can be assumed to consume more CDs then the average of the population. 30-50% is therefore a more reasonable estimate.

Summarizing both papers and linking it to the sub reasearch question from the introduction which is 'What has the effect of the internet been on the laws and economy of the music industry?' a few conclusions can be drawn:
- Illegal downloading and piracy does contribute marginally to reduction of profits that are made in the music industry
- More laws seem to be needed, because there are more and easier way to illegally steal music with good quality
- Different business plans are needed because the internet provides different ways to distribute and sell products
- Just laws to protect copyrights are not enough. They can be supported by technological measures

In the just mentioned list of statements, the word 'seem' has purposely been used in the second of them. So far a lot of copyright acts and new laws have been implemented to deal with issues to arise because of the internet. It is until now however not clearly been proven that their implementation has any use or that they are just implemented as a natural reaction to an unwanted situation. In the next chapter the effectiveness of international copyright conventions is therefore assessed.
## International copyright conventions and the music industry
Since the current approach with laws and protection turn out to not be effective enough as shown in the previous chapter, a different approach need to be taken. The internet is not bounded to a country and therefore an approach in which are regulated internationally might be feasible. The paper named 'How effective are international copyright conventions in the music industry'[7] deals with this approach. This approach takes two steps. First a brief history of the international copyright legislation in the music industry is being assessed, after which the effectiveness of it is empirically determined. Since the paper is from 1996, it deals with the era until the internet really became popular, but lessons learned from technological changes can be generalized and applied to anticipate on future developments.

In 1933 the International Federation of Phonogram Producers was founded, an organization which aims on defending the international domain of interests by preserving their rights. This is done by creating legislation in countries they do not exist and safeguarding the laws in countries they do. This organization was needed to fill up the gap the was there because of the absence of governments to protect intellectual property rights relating to music.

However in the 1930s the absence of parallel and uniform legislation regarding copyright caused several problems:
- Reduced incentive to export their recordings
- Reduced incentive for a domestic firm to set up a subsidiary in a foreign country
- Two companies in different countries buying the rights for a piece of work, but then on company also attending the market of the other company and thereby reducing the market share of the latter country
Another treat included pirate record companies that violated property rights by producing unlicensed pre-recorded software.

In 1961 the Rome convention caused a major change. It was the first time both phonograph producers and performers were to be given on an international level. For producers this included reproduction rights and performance rights, while for the performers this implied performers this meant income anytime the work was used in any way. This seemed to be a successful way to go. Later in the 1960s new inventions caused problems for enforcing the rights. The invention of the two-track cassette tape by Philips made it possible to copy music in relatively high quality and in small amounts involving low costs. The latter property caused this technology to be available for everyone. The portability of this technology made it possible to take a tape recorder to concerts to tape the performance. Although the quality might be low, real enthusiastic people were still very happy with this product. Also the durability and the easy with which the product could be transported contributed positively to the level of piracy. The effect was made even worse in 1969 when Dolby Noise reduction innovations caused the quality of the recordings to be higher and when in 1983 the introduction of the Philips-Sony compact Disc in 1983 increased the quality of tape recordings. Sony's DAT and Philips' DCC technology made it possible to even record digitally shortly after this.

A more advanced legislation was needed because of the just mentioned technological improvements, which came in June 1989. The agreement became known as the Athens agreement and stated that the European countries accepted the principle of royalties and will not oppose efforts by the Recording industry to secure legislation to implement such royalties. Because of this legislation governments were involved in the process of fighting piracy, which made it much easier to implement legislation fighting it.
The effectiveness of the copyright laws is assessed by comparing different countries that have and have not implemented them. Out of the countries that have hardly any piracy 94%, 56% and 67% implemented respectively the Berne, Rome and Phonogram enactments. For countries with a medium amount of piracy the numbers are 69%, 38% and 44%. Finally the countries that are overwhelmed with piracy show the numbers 47%, 37% and 58%. Although there seems to be a small negative correlation between the piracy level and the enactments implemented, the result can be explained much better by introducing a third variable. This variable is the national income of a country. It turns out that in countries in which there is little piracy, the national income is generally very high and vice versa. This is supported by statistical analysis, but this does not change the point that the international laws are not effective in fighting piracy. This answers the last sub research question which was 'How effective are international copyright conventions in the music industry?'.


## Internet and laws and policies to regulate information streams
Now that the four sub research question are answered in the previous four chapter, it is possible to look at the answers and answer the main research question which is as mentioned in the introduction: 'What impact does the internet and its ability to spread information quickly and massively have on the laws and policies that are there to regulate information streams from a moral perspective?'

With respect to the first sub research question in which the medium used and whom having the power plays a role, different eras have been identified. Every era lead to more possibilities with respect to music. The quality increased and power came into the hands of the consumer. The second sub research question adds to this that the distribution speed. and costs dropped. From a utilitarian point of view this is a very desirable situation. Higher quality means that people will individually experience more satisfaction and for the sum of these individuals it means the whole world becomes a better place. With respect to costs and distribution speeds a utilitarian point of view is also very good. Lower costs means that people can afford more products for the same work and a higher distribution speed causes a product to be used quicker after the wish for a product occurs. The power shift from big companies and governments that make the law to the consumer being in charge of the market can be seen from different ethical point of views. Ethical egoism is about maintaining yourself and when the customer can get exactly what he wants, this is clearly a good thing. Utilitarians want the world in total to be a better place, but in the case a single customer has a lot of power, his individual goals might compromise what is good for everyone. For example when a customer wants a very unique product and the company needs to offer it for a good price in order to keep its name clean. The company will have to do a lot of effort to make this product which can only be sold to one person and therefore can offer a reduced service to other customers for the same efforts. Finally from the ethics of duty one can state the a company should take go care of its customers, no matter what they ask for. When the power comes in the hand of the customers, this clearly becomes the case.


Looking at the effects of the internet on the laws and economy of the music industry, results are initially not so promising. First of all piracy has empirically be proven to contribute to a loss of revenue. When the ethical egoism theory is applied here, it does make sense that people attend in piracy activities. The chance to be caught is small, while the benefits of stealing music are relatively marginal. On average attending in piracy activities will thus benefit an individual. However, when people have a conscience or when people respect that an author or producer should make money, the ethics of respect or conscience counterfeit this statement. Since the internet technology is still very new, it is still developing in the concepts of people's minds. It might therefore be the case that people do not experience their actions as stealing, because they are not taking away a physical product from someone. Laws are created more and more but do not turn out to have a desired effect as was found out when answering sub research question four. Technological measures are set in place as well, but are generally cracked again. The way to achieve most benefit is to change the business plans drastically. New technology offers new possibilities and different ways in making revenues. When the consumer is centered in the new plans and when piracy does not lead to much benefit compared to the efforts that need to be made for it, it will become the case that the revenue streams are guaranteed. In addition to this the conceptual attitude towards downloading and behaving on the internet can be changed. This can be done in several ways which support each other:
- Virtue ethics can play a role by having examples of heroes (such as David Beckham in football) state that downloading music illegally is not cool.
- Changing the concept of downloading illegally from the internet can be changed in a similar way as the word hacker is associated with negative properties now.
- Ethics of duty can contribute to how a normal person should behave in this world
- When the overall welfare in the world improves, piracy will drop as well as the fourth sub research question's answer shows

What is mentioned in the previous two paragraphs does not only count for the music industry, but any kind of information that has value and for which the author should at least be rewarded for creating it. This reward is needed in order to ensure that in the future there is still motivation to create valuable information or knowledge. From the first paragraph one can conclude that technology in potential only brings good, since more having more options potentially means having more welfare. Also the point that the customer is in charge generally is a good thing. The second paragraph has some contradictions about morality. Right now revenue streams of the music industry are dropping, but having the right business plans and making sure that people get the right ethical attitudes toward the internet can stop this. The general conclusion therefore is that even if it would be possible to stop the technological developments, it is morally best to continue developing them and that people will always find a way to deal with new technologies.
## Conclusion
This paper has made an effort to answer the research question 'What impact does the internet and its ability to spread information quickly and massively have on the laws and policies that are there to regulate information streams from a moral perspective?'. The process of answering the question involved four sub questions which are answered through a literature study. The sub questions deal with the evolution of the music industry, how the internet has changed trading and distribution, what impact the internet has had on laws and economy of the music industry and what the effect is of international laws with respect to fighting privacy. It turns out that there are four eras in the music industry, in which in the last era the consumer has the power because of the internet. This means a approach in which the consumer is centralized is needed. This is supported by the answer of the second sub research question, which deals with how e-commerce companies should be organized. 
The third research question's answer shows the piracy has effected the revenue streams of the music industry negatively and that there have been technological and legal attempts to counterfeit this numerously without always achieving the desired result. The fourth sub question confirms this suspicion by empirically showing that countries that have enacted international laws against piracy do not have a lower piracy rate. Instead of that piracy rates seem to depend on the national income, in which richer countries show lower piracy rates. The parts that deal with the music industry can be generalized to the whole 
e-commerce sector especially the companies that sell information.

The answers of the four sub research questions have been reviewed from a moral point of view. This leads to two major conclusions:
- Technological improvements lead to more options about what to offer to the customer and thus will improve the overall welfare and are in a utilitarian view thus morally very welcome
- The consumer gets more power and this takes away power from the traditional authorities which can involve risks
The former statement is a morally positive one but the second one involves some risks, such as making it to attend in piracy activities much easier and thus more tempting. There are a few solutions to this which include setting example heroes through virtue ethics, changing the concept of illegally downloading more into stealing, making it common sense to just buy anything with copyright you need and having the welfare improve all over the world which reduces the need for stealing. The final conclusion from these two points is that even when it is possible to stop technologies such as the internet, it is not wanted to do so because morally they are a positive phenomenon.
