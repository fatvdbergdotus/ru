## Argumentation schedule
Marvin Minsky starts his paper by stating that he believes mind and body are the same thing. This statement is supported by stating that seeing them as separate entities would create more problems to explain, then solving any. In order to give his position more strength, Minsky mentions eight points in which he either defends his position by breaking down arguments of the opposition or tries to make clear why his position make sense.

The first three arguments deal with memory and change. Firstly [A] it being hard to hard to believe that thinking is an aspect of the brain. It seems hard to believe that the thinking processes we engage in can be written down in physical laws. However, Minsky defends this by stating that these processes are still very vague to us and that we do not have the right knowledge available yet to even have a clue about what is going on. 

Secondly [B] Minsky mentions something about feeling we have some sort of Self that remains unchanged. This makes the subject of having a memory and being someone hard, since we have the feeling that we're always the same person at any point in time. This is however an illusion according to Minsky, since when we compare ourselves now as an adult compared to being with a child, a completely different person can be seen. Another aspect to make his statement stronger is done by stressing out that we think about machines in terms how they affect materials and that our brains (even hardly ever thought of in that way) are basically doing the same while engaged in thinking and sensing processes.

Thirdly [C] Minsky uses a metaphor with a computer. He states that the experience we have gained with computers has given us a lot of understanding in clarifying the mind-body problem. He compares our brains with software, which used to have a separation between data and programs, but later evolved into programs writing new programs and programs even changing themselves. In Minsky's mind this is what is happening in our brain as well and there is no fundamental difference between acquiring new data versus acquiring new processes. He states in addition to this that it is still possible to use the concept of mind, but only when we clearly define it in terms of the actually underlying processes we are referring to. This concludes the part about memory and change.


Next the embodiment of minds is discussed by Minsky. In his fourth argument [D] Minsky talks about thinking that we can easily transfer what is on our mind to some other entity or someone else. In Minsky's eyes this is caused by minds being mostly concerned about changes in states, which often do not deal with the states themselves. As examples he mentions the functional view in which we only care how an agent acts in a certain state and a computer in which signals represent information. It is therefore possible to copy things that are there, as long as they are copied in such a way which makes them do the same. Therefore it does not matter that we somehow have the feeling that our minds can exist separately from the physical.

Continuing with the embodiment having a Self seems a big issue to Minsky [E]. In his eyes this is just an elaborately constructed illusion. He does not deny that this vision has a big practical value though, but he mentions this vision only exists to see the effect we have in the world and to reflect upon our actions. Once again he mentions that we actually change a lot.

As a final part of the embodiment [F] the ability to draw conclusions and fantasizing about the world we live in, seems something so far from what could actually be happening in the world that this activity seems hard to take place in the actual world. As an adult this seems that case, but as Minsky states, logical thinking is not so straightforward and trustworthy for children. Also when we as adults suffer from brain damage, are intoxicated or exhausted, the ability to reason well disappears. Note that the previously mentioned things are all physical things If logic would be really something going in the mind, explaining why thing are going wrong would be a very hard thing to do.

About the freedom of will issue[G], Minsky deals with complex machinery and quantum mechanics. Although often quantum mechanics is embraced for leaving space open for the freedom of will, Minsky states exactly the opposite. According to him the quantum mechanics has made everything more exact then it has ever been. 

Also in addressing uncertainty and stability [H] quantum mechanics is an issue stressed by Minsky. Compared to things at larger scale, such as planets, randomness is completely diminished. Of course, some aspects of quantum mechanics are based on probabilistic models, the results are however very accurate. Compared to the 'old' laws of physics, stable structures can remain for millions of years.


## Linkage to literature
In this paragraph everything that has been mentioned is linked to the current literature available. Minsky starts his paper clearly by saying mind and body are the same thing, namely some physical process. Although he does not explicitly mention it, his way of talking indicates that he is someone embracing physicalism. Taking this stance means that Minsky propagates the following key points:
    • Everything is physical
    • A complete physics will explain all the facts about the world
    • Psychological, biological or social features of the world supervene on physical facts about the world

Also the functionalism point of view seems to fit Minsky's point made in the paper. In functionalism something happens because the system is organized in a certain way, but the physical state of the system is just an aim to achieve this reaction.
The continuation of this paragraph will deal with recognizing the previously mentioned points in the argumentation schedule as well as referring to functionalism. In addition to this dealing with the rejection of arguments of people taking another position then Minsky is being taken care of.

Starting with the first argument [A] about it being hard to believe that thinking is part of the brain. There is not enough knowledge available here according to Minsky. Somehow this is considered a weak reason, because if we do not know enough about something, different explanations could also be valid. Or as Descartes states, the mind is a separate substance from the body (dualism) can not be proven false then. Descartes knows that he is thinking, but he is not sure about having a body, therefore he sees these things separated. But also this does not need to be the case, since insecurity once again leaves all the options open. Therefore from both positions there is not convincing argument on this point.

Next the feeling of some self is challenged [B]. Descartes describes this as: “I think therefore I exist” and also Minsky gives an examples as “I remember X”. According to Minsky this is caused by thinking about ourselves as remaining unchanged. The convincing example he states here is about people changing. This does however not explain why we can feel some sort of self and why we feel as if we are in control of all our decisions. Of course this could merely be an illusion, since the environment has a lot of impact on what we can and can not do. Berkely's Idealism deal with this by putting something between our mind and a god on the other side. Everything that is going on then, I just created in our mind, such as happens in the matrix movie with an illusionary world. This is supported by Eliminative Materialism, in which theories get replaced by newer ones (falsification) depending on what we experience.

As a final part of the memory and change paragraph[C], Minsky makes a comparison with a computer. The good thing about this metaphor is that at least it is based on something we have knowledge about. Even when our minds do not work the way Minsky proposes, the knowledge we have about computers can be an excellent tool for dealing with the real world and running simulations that appear to have the same effect as our brains. The functionalism approach fits in here very well, since it states that if the results of two calculations are the same, the calculations can be considered the same. Functionalism does not state anything about whether a computer can do what a human can do. Physicalism implicitly say it is possible. When dualism is applied to the computer metaphor, a clash occurs, because the dualism approaches state that the mind is separated from the body. This means that no matter how hard we try to make a copy of something that has the same functions, we will fail, because we can not copy the mind. So far we have not been able to copy the functions and behaviour of a human to another entity and therefore it is hard to make statements about this aspect.

Continuing with the embodiment[D] Minsky talks about functionalism. In his eyes it seems (potentially) possible to copy something from a human and have it operate in a different physical form but still producing the same results as functionalism states. This is also called multiple realizability. This has never done before and therefore until the validation or falsification of the statement has been done will remain insecure. From the dualistic point of view this seems impossible, since there are two substances which are called mind and body. As far as we can see the mind is not directly accessible and can definitely not be copied from this point of view. The question that one often wonders here is how mind and body connect, since we seem to have defined things pretty well by the laws of physics.
Having a self [E] is something that is easily waved away by Minsky as being an illusion. I think this is a point in which Minsky is going a bit too fast, because from our experience we are in control and we seem to sense and act in the world around us. Creating a mind here seems to solve the problem, but as stated before this introduces many new problems. What makes more sense here is the statements Irrealism makes, in which the mind observes some ideas, which are triggered by something called god.
This theory really reserves some space for something we at least experience to be. The only point this theory leaves in the middle is whether there is an objective truth that goes beyond inter subjectivity. When there is, this could be part of the element named god in the model.

About reasoning in the world we attend in [F] Minsky mentions that this could be part of the world and that we definitely are not born with logical thinking skills. I think the point he makes here is pretty clear and also by illustrating that we do not think clearly when we are drunk or brain damaged, convincing link between the physical (body) and mental world (mind) is made.

About freedom of will [G] quantum mechanics is introduced supposing to make everything exact. I don't agree on this point, since we are not sure why things are behaving a certain way on such a small scale. It is however defendable that if we would have the right tools and observation, that insecurity does not exist. Once again Irrealism can support this by the notion of a god and physicism by expressing the state of the world in terms of material.

Finally uncertainty and stability [H]. Minsky states that quantum mechanics can cause more stable structures then we have ever seen before with the laws of Newton. It is however questionable why the structures even need to exist, since the world around us seems to change rapidly. In other theories these things are not really taken into account very much.

What Minsky has left out of the picture is the brain in a vat or matrix theory, which states that everything we see and experience can just be an illusion. Since this can not be denied philosophically, Minsky can by just observing the world and making statements be proven wrong in some matter.

## My own substantiated opinion
This paper is concluded with my own opinion on the mind body problem. First of all one might wonder why to bother about it at all. I have come to the conclusion that especially in four areas it is important to know what is going, namely:
- Philosophically: Who is morally responsible for things that happen?
- Physics: Why do things happen around us the way they do?
- Legally: What should we do to make the world a better and more fair place?
- AI: Who is accountable for things software cause?

Ultimately we would like to have some sort of uniform approach here, but as discovered earlier it seems not possible to know every single detail of what is happening in the world, even when we know the meta model of the world (read: the way the world is working and what causes what). Therefore I advocate different approaches to deal with different problems. I will first state my own belief on what is happening in the world with respect to the mind body problem, after which I will mention different approaches for different areas of field.

As a person that attaches value to mathematics my ultimate wish and therefore also belief until proven wrong, is that everything that happens in the world can be expressed in numbers. This approach is very functional and means that once we have enough details about something, we can predict what is going to happen. A very clear example in practice of this, is a computer. Even though many small processes happen and relatively complex calculations are the result, a certain computer program produces the same result on different computers. Physicism plays another role here too, since everything the computer does is a result of the laws of focus, but I prefer not to focus on this aspect, because there are many ways to realize a computer that does the same. In the worst case we could have a human performing the calculation a computer does manually. Although this might be (very much) slower then a computer, the results are still there.

Philosophically the outcome of what I have described just now might be very dramatic. When everything I just a big calculation, the future might be fixed and no matter what people do, the result might already be predetermined. Somehow this would be very easy, because there would be no moral responsibility, expect for the one (maybe god?) who has started this whole process.

From the physics stance I think we should just see what we are able to observe and not observe and see what regularities we can find there. This can be done independent of what one beliefs. However we can see that the science is driven by people who are powerful in some sort of way. This can be either by status (a lot of citations) or by money (possibility to hire a lot of people that do research for you or mislead the big crowd). Discoveries in physics might lead to one of more theories proven right or wrong with respect to the mind body theory, but until now we do not have any prove for any of them.

Next the legal system perspective. The legal system is some system besides the real world, which prevents very unwanted things to happen. This system does not always need to focus on what is right or wrong, but should mostly provide a safe feeling for citizens and prevent a small group to have too much control. With respect to the mind body problem it is important to keep different theories in mind, but mostly focus on what has practically turned out to work satisfactory. Evolutionary approaches seem better here in which the legal system slowly adapts over time to keep up with the real world. Philosophers can be drivers here in order to make it go the right direction.

Finally the mind body problem with respect to AI. Just like the real world computers can be very complex systems and software can be used or have results that the developer never intended it to have. I think it is important here to see what the developer could have known but also to accept here that involving software to solve a problem includes some risk. Therefore I think the focus here should be on functionalism in which it is clearly defined what a piece of software does. This goes against the beliefs of soft AI, such as neural networks, in which software seemingly seems to operate well, but no one truly knows why. Examples here are: Google returning the wanted results to us and Tomtom leading us to the location we want to go to. In my eyes it seems best to minimize the impact these systems can have on our lives and never uses these approaches in critical systems.
