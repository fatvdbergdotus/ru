## Introduction
ICT has given people the opportunity to take an identity on-line, that doesn't need to match their real world's identity. Asai (2007) has reported about this phenomenon in on-line computer games in Japan. It turned out that in these games the woman/man ratio was about one to one, while 91% of the people playing these games were man. Some simple mathematics show that many men impersonate to be a woman in this game. The numbers make it hard to say something about women impersonating a man, since this group is too small to judge. Different reasons and possible explanations have been given for this and shall be discussed in this paper. The paper deals with the just mentioned and performs a case study from the ethical perspective on it, since somehow the moral of being honest is being disrespected. Different ethical theories will be compared and applied to this case resulting in a generally defined judgement for this behaviour.


## Motives
Different ethical theories have different views on a certain event happening, but a more general conceptual view then these theories is the separation being made between intentions and actions. This means, what the person being judged actually wanted to happen versus what the person being judged caused to happen. In the current case study what is happening can be investigated by going into the on-line games, tap on private chats, interview people on their experiences and other ways. 

The intentions are somewhat harder to discover, but research has revealed some:
- guys expressing their feminine side which needs to be suppressed in real life
- guys gaining access to a female only group in the on-line game
- guys tricking other males into making revelations and then embarrass them with that
- guys exploring reactions of males to the other gender

These four aspects are still very general concepts and could be specified as much as one wishes. For example, taking a look at the last intention show that reactions are being explored. But what's the deeper meaning of knowing this reactions. Some random explanations are: a guy is unsure and wants to figure out how other males deal with women, a guy never gets attention while by being a woman in an on-line game suddenly many people talk to him and finding out if guys generally treat women nice out of curiosity.


## Different ethical frameworks
In order to take a clear look at the situation just sketched some ethical frameworks will be shortly introduced after which each one of the will be applied to the case separately.

First there are the divine command theories. They say whatever is told by the Bible, Qur'an or any other holy sacrament is the way to behave. So if you behave a certain way and someone judges you, you can refer to one of those documents to justify your behaviour. This results into 3 problems: Things that are written down can be interpreted in different ways since natural languages is ambiguous. The different documents can have opposite opinions about how to behave which makes it impossible to say which one to follow. What sacrament is considered to be holy enough to follow or not and who decides this.

Second there is the ethics of our inner voice. The main point here is that our conscience will tell us how to behave. When someone is raised religious, it might show much overlap with the previous framework. This theory has the following problems: How can one be sure that someone's conscience is reliable? For example a lot of alcohol consumption or intoxication in any other way might temporarily lead to different results on this area. Besides that it might be hard or likely impossible to check whether someone has been following their conscience, making it impossible to express any justified judgement at all. 

Third there's ethical egoism. This theory implies that everything you do should eventually benefit yourself in some way, either being in the short or long run. Although this theory might seem very radical, selfish and negative at first, results in multi agent computing with selfish agents have shown remarkable results in cooperation and achieving things together. In this case people can only be judged for not being selfish enough and letting others walk over them.

Fourth there's utilitarianism. Its key point is that people should behave in such a way to reduce suffering in general and cause as much good and happiness as possible to the world in general. So unlike the ethical egoism it's allowed to sometimes sacrifice yourself a little as long as it leads to a better cause in general and although they both might seem opposites, they are somehow strongly related, because some people believe that utilitarianism is somehow the same as ethical egoism since acting good will make people like you and eventually pay of for you as well (this is expressed in a conceptual view called the prisoner's dilemma).

Finally there's the ethics of respect. Respect means that you should act in such a way that makes others comfortable with you. This is often a general thing in culture but also depends a lot on how you expect that the other expects you to behave. Finding out what other people want first is the main key point here.


## Study 1: Devine command theories

They are both big books and different parts could be applied to the case study and even within one book such as the Bible one might find pros and cons for the impersonating a different gender behaviour on-line. In order to make the case study practical on this topic, without having to read all the holy books, the well known 10 commandments have been chosen as guidelines for the case. First a selection has been made in the commandments of ones that can directly be applied. This results in number 6 (Neither shall you commit adultery), number 7 (you shall not steal) and number 9 (You shall not bear false witness against your neighbour) being selected. Depending on the motives, one or more of the above commandments apply. The you shall not steal commandment can be used in case of tricking another person in providing information (the 3rd and 4th intention mentioned in the motives chapter), since one tries to get access to information that doesn't belong to him and thus steals it. Commandment 9 is the case in every situation since lying is always the case, because one can't really deny knowing his real gender. Finally commandment number 6 which is in case of the bible taken very strictly and includes even thinking of anything close to adultery. Since the case studies deals with gender motives some cases might involve intentions of adultery. Since commandment number 9 includes anything, no matter what intention is given and since the commandments are commonly used (not just for Christian religions) it's fair to conclude that when a Devine command perspective on ethics is taken, that the behaviour of the case study is not acceptable no matter what.

## Study 3: Ethical egoism
The ethical egoism assumes everyone should take care of themselves. From this viewpoint people should behave in a way that benefits them most, perhaps regardless of the effects they have on other people (except when making someone angry at you might have a negative effect someday or directly in return of course).  When this is linked to the case study, every motive can be justified in some way as long as the person either feels better from it or gets control of resources (information, money etc.) from it. Motive one about showing the feminine side can be justified by causing some emotional relief from it. When certain feelings are suppressed for a long time or can't be expressed at all in society, it might hurt a person a lot. By being a female in virtual way one can feel better. The other three motives focus on gaining information in some way. In which the second one takes care of getting access to new resources by joining a female sphere and the last two ones by direct interaction. As briefly mentioned before one could address some cons as well: one can commit illegal actions and be disadvantaged by law or being deleted from the game, which is from the egoistic perspective not the best option one can choose for itself. Besides that making people angry can have negative consequences for that person in the long run, because a person that's hated always has to be aware for danger happening (This is a Buddhist point of view on the topic) 


## Study 4: Utilitarianism
As stated in the introduction of the frameworks, utilitarianism is mostly about making the world a better place. In comparison to ethical egoism enforcing this way of behaving is very complex. The first problem that arises with it, that it seems virtually impossible to make a utility function for the whole universe. For example: what if you can help someone very much, but it will hurt you in some way, what do you need to do then? If many people would be sacrificing themselves for a bigger cause, we might end up in a world with unhappy people only. Applying this view to the case study motive 3 and 4 in which people are fooled and are treated in a way they are likely not wanting to be treated, are against the main principles of utilitarianism. In case of motive one about expressing the feminine side, a view on deeper intentions is needed. When a person just likes to express a certain side of himself and even tells others that he's actually a man, one might consider the common well-being no so disadvantaged, but when on the other hand the feminine appearance leads to other guys emotionally getting attached to the avatar and the person behind it, one has to consider this behaviour unacceptable from the utilitarian point of view. That leaves the second motive about gaining access to a female populated sphere. From the utilitarian perspective this motive is unconditionally unacceptable, because the reason the restriction is put on the sphere is because the people taking part in it, don't want guys to enter it. This could be because they want to share information that's embarrassing to men or just want to talk in a different way. No matter what the exact reasons are, the restriction expresses the wish not to be bothered by guys.
