### Introduction
This report will discuss the paper 'Rethinking Politics in the World of ICTs' by Sara Bentivegna. After providing an argumentation schedule, the paper is being critically overlooked with respect to the current literature and ideas about this issue. In order to display this situation the paper is split in four major parts. The first part is about a global view on politics and ICT, the second about politics entering the online territory, the third about the influence of ICT on the shaping and creation of public spheres and the final part about the interaction politics and ICT engage in. Every part will be discussed in a separate paragraph in the continuation of this report, including both the information being provided by the paper on the corresponding topic and  the reflection with regard to current literature and ideas. ### ICTs and politics
First the way ICTs and politics are generally seen. Bentivegna's paper starts with mentioning that the process of ICTs and politics getting involved with each other has already been going on for 40 years, although in the early phase them interacting was seen as a utopia and perhaps something never ever happening. Politics is being described as an element of human nature which has and always been there, and ICTs are being seen as a human tool having the effect in this context making it hard to describe the human's reading and writing activities. This induces combining ICT and politics in any kind of way makes it very hard to analyse what's going on. The writer puts serious doubts whether we as humans can actually see how ICT has reshaped politics because of the previously mentioned. For example, maybe we've been very conservative in our idea of politics and a change of way of thinking was going to happen after all, no matter how much ICT has been and is going to be involved. This is exclaimed in the final part of the paragraph in which Bentivegna states that in order to understand ICT and politics, first a new understanding about politics is mandatory.  With regard to politics the mass parties have disappeared and became so called catch all parties, personal parties etc. Reinventing politics is what needs to be done according to Bentivegna.   
Comparing the views of Bentivegna with the paper “Digitale Democratie” (dutch for digital democracy) of Jan A.G.M. Van Dijk some conclusions could be driven. Van Dijk agrees on the point that it's very hard to describe what's going on between ICT and politics, but does offer a list of 3 things ICTs can and have meant to politics so far, namely: ICTs improve communications about political information between all parties involved, ICTs support debates and negotiation and ICTs increases the participation of civilians in politics. This list is also being described by Bentivegna in the paragraph about politicians going online. Just like Bentivegna, Van Dijk is very sceptical about what ICTs could mean for politics. He illustrates this by all the deceptions we've experienced so far in previously done in inventions, such as electricity, radio, newspaper, phone and television, which is of course very arguable though (hard to measure, what would the world look like without those inventions etc.).



### Online politics
Second the issue of politics getting online is being reviewed. This process is causing great expectations from the ICT. Bentivegna mentions that it's clear that democracy has been going on for several decades and that a lot of good things have came from it, but in contrast to that he mentions that people are loosing faith in democracy. This is caused by all the promises democracy made to the people, which simply couldn't and can't be solved, either completely and/or instantly. Some examples are: crime, drugs, traffic accidents and pollution. This causes a separation between politics and citizens, because of the false promises. Bentivegna links to several literature sources to support her words on this. In order to bridge this gap, ICTs are very well welcomed by politicians. Right now complex questions about what to offer with the ICTs possibilities need to be answered and Bentivegna also states that the changes on this area have had major impact on and causing changes to the traditional system of political communication. Quotes are made of Levy and Clift to state that ICTs will save democracy like tv has (notice the contrast with Van Dijk) and dictorial regimes will be swept away by it. This is being questioned by trying to understand what definition of democracy is being used of which the options are: representive, direct and strong democracy. Every kind offers different options in which ICTs can mean something. This is very parallel to Van Dijk's previously mentioned paper in which four distinct ICT task categories for politics are being defined: Decision making, opinion forming, asking opinions and providing information. Linking this together a representative democracy allows mostly space for information providing, a direct democracy has in addition to this participation (asking opinions and some decision making) and in a strong democracy all the four aspects are being fulfilled. Unlike Zouridis' paper named “From street level to system level democracies” no information is being provided about how these ICTs applications change the interaction between government and citizens. Zouridis talks about different views in terms of street level, screen level and system level. A major example to show the differences is the application for something in the town hall (physical interface with government at street level) compared to the application online (virtual interface at screen level). When a citizen enters information on the internet and applies, everything that happends after that in time, is completely out of his control and sight, while in case of a physical interface direct human contact is being made, resulting in better options for feedback (getting angry, coming back every day to ask about the progress, making sure the right person is at your case). On the other hand efficiency of a much higher rate can be achieved in the online interface.


### Political spheres
Third the shaping and creation of political spheres. Bentivegna starts with clearly explaining what a sphere is, which can be seen as some sort of community interacting but also very general communities with one way communication traffic can be included. Some examples are: a television channel, a newspaper, public spaces such as waiting rooms, internet websites, rss feeds and email lists. Linking to Dahlin Bentivegna results in stating there are three dimensions in all these spheres, namely structures, representation and interaction in which the last two are mostly affected by ICTs. Representation by how information can be represented (eg. Reading a website is very different then reading a book because of its non linear structure) and interaction by how communication between humans is done (ICTs offer communication regardless of time, location and amount of information being spread). After that a separation between public and policy spheres are being suggested in which former one focuses on sharing feelings, values, experiences without any obligation or goal and the latter one focuses on translating feelings, values and believes to political exponents. The transformation of these spheres is highly appreciated by Bentivegna, but in contrast to that Van Dijk shows serious doubt in his paper about this transformation process. He offers some reasons for this, including: people using ICTs don't represent the whole population well (still the majority of internet users is male for example), a very small part of the ICT users is involved in political (related) activities online, people using a computer don't like acting social (because they are interacting with a device and not a human) while the political spheres are a social happening and finally and perhaps worst the quality of the interaction and information in these spheres is often very low (people only post a message once, opinions vary much and don't come closer to each other resulting in no agreement being made). Some issues like the distribution of ICT users might evolve over time, but some reasons require an explicit solution. Linking back to the previous paragraph and also being confirmed by Van Dijk is the influence of these spheres on politics. Most developed countries (perhaps even all) deal with a representative democracy in which there's mostly space for providing information by ICTs and Van Dijk adds to this that the impact of the ICTs haven't had a significant contribution to political decision making, although it's important to realise that Van Dijk's paper mostly deals with the nineties.


### ICTs and politics interact
Finally the way ICTs and politics interact. What's mostly being discussed by Bentivegna are ICTs changing politics on one hand but also being objects of change at the same time at the other hand. This makes it very hard to analyse what's going on in general. ICTs can have a valueable contribution to politics, but only smart innovative ICT ideas only can achieve that status. The problem is that ICTs are being overestimed instead of seeing them as a tool with which humans have new options. Politics are in a post democratic status as mentioned in the paragraph about ICT and politics and ICTs might play a role in stopping this process. The paper of Snellen named “electronic governance” adds some valuable information to this. A three phase plan is being presented in which ICT contributes to politics, namely a pre-implementation, implementation and post-implementation phase. All three phases are described an a concrete example paying off. This example is about the city of Delft in the Netherlands cashing 80 million guilders by a sale (about 35 million euros) and letting the citizens decide how to spend it. In the pre-implementation phase ideas are being brainstormed at city and neighbourhood level, after which the money was actually spend in a democratic way in the next phase.
