## Introduction

This report will deal with the effects of teamwork on idea generation with respect to innovative performance. Three scientific papers are used in a short literature study, after which it becomes possible to state which conditions concerning teamwork are needed to create a positive effect. These statements can be translated to a metric that is measurable, after which concrete advice with respect to the design of teams can be given.

## Literature

The first paper of the three [1] deals with two aspects being important namely attention and incubation. Doing an empirical experiment in the paper, the hypotheses are confirmed about them being important. The attention being important implies that when people look at each other's ideas, a positive effect on their own idea making occurs. In other words, when people get to see each other's ideas, the sum of the ideas the group generates is bigger than the sum a group of individually working people generates. This is due to an additional cognitive stimulus. With respect to incubation one can state that a person should also have the ability to comment on others' ideas. The paper however shows that both sessions should happen subsequently.

The second paper [2] compares FTF (face-to-face) communication with CMC (computer mediated communication). The technique used however is not the striking aspect of this paper, but the notion of anonymity contributing to idea creation is. Participants of the experiment found more satisfaction when anonymity of idea creation was the case. The main advantage of anonymity is that everyone contributing is treated equally, independent of the power they have in the organization (money, position, social skills etc.) and thus only the quality of the new ideas is being judged.

The final paper [3] deals with SECI, a four-step program for knowledge creation. Important here is that in order to create the best ideas, brainstorming teams should be cross-functional and thus contain people with different backgrounds. This will make it more likely that the people can complement each other and that ideas that are hard to imitate by competition will arise. Also the four processes named socializing, externalization, connecting and embodying should be practiced within the different members of the organization, meaning that knowledge should be shared tacitly after which people can think of new ideas theoretically and then translate this back to tacit knowledge again.

## Metric

From the literature study in the previous paragraph, it becomes now possible to tell what one should focus on to let teamwork contribute to generating new ideas by defining different metrics:

- **Attention level:** The percentage of people having access and spending time on thinking about an idea someone has come up with.

- **Reflection level:** The percentage of people reflecting and giving feedback to someone's idea.

- **Equality in contributing ideas:** The equality level of the two previous metrics per person generating ideas. (e.g. when a manager comes up with a new idea he might get more attention than an employee coming up with an idea, which is unwanted.)

- **SECI:** The number of formal processes available to support the four SECI knowledge creation processes.

- **Cross-functional teams:** The number of team members per department in the idea-generating team.

The final result of these metrics can be measured by counting the number of ideas that are actually implemented per month.

## Managerial Implications

For managers the just mentioned metrics have the following consequences:

- A team should be set up for idea generation consisting of members from as many different departments as possible.

- The team members should work with each other both practically and theoretically.

- Formal procedures have to be put in place to guarantee that members will look at each other's ideas and give feedback about them.

- Every team member should be treated equally, independent of the power they have in the company.

## Conclusion

A literature study has been done on teamwork affecting idea generation with respect to innovation. This has led to some statements that are important in order to have teamwork contribute the most to idea generation. Attention, incubation, equality, cross-functional teams, and SECI turn out to be the important keywords here which have been translated to metrics. From these words managerial implications have been derived, resulting in four statements managers should follow to support idea generation.
