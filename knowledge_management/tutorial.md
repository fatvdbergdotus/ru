## Freek van den Berg s0517593: Knowledge Management Tutorial 1

This report is written for the subject Knowledge Management at the Radboud University Nijmegen, The Netherlands. The paper 'Knowledge makes a difference: knowledge as strategic capability' by Michael Zack from 1999 is being analyzed and discussed here. First the objectives of the paper will be discussed, followed by the research path and key elements of the argument, concluded by the relevance of the paper both to society as well as science. Every just mentioned aspect will be dealt with from the writer's view as well as my personal critical assessment on it.

Starting with the objectives of the paper. The writer starts with stating that business organizations see knowledge as their most valuable and strategic resource. This is emphasized later in the paper by making assumable that the more an organization knows about its costumers, products, technologies etc., the better it should be able to perform.
The writer continues with linking to his previous research, which concludes that the most important context for guiding knowledge is the firm's strategy. This calls for more attention on a firm's strategy with respect to knowledge.
Third many companies struggle with expressing the link between their strategy and its intellectual resources and capabilities. This means that the knowledge an organization has is potentially not fully used in the strategy it takes and that the strategy doesn't fully support the knowledge processes.
Fourth organizations are unable to measure and evaluate the benefits of the efforts taken with respect to managing and developing the right knowledge. 
All above objectives could be generalized to one main objective which is: How can the currently available knowledge and the chosen business strategy maximally support each other? This is what is referred to as knowledge strategy by Zack.

In my opinion Zack states what the problems are very clearly. This is done by linking to his previous research as well as linking to other papers. He shows there's an importance for using knowledge and linking it to strategy. However, the main objective of the paper is not explicitly expressed and can only be determined by combining the problems given by Zack. Besides that Zack lacks showing different perspectives on knowledge and its importance with respect to business strategy. One could think of people stating that business strategy is completely independent of knowledge for example.

In order to achieve its objectives, Zack roughly takes five steps. First an explanation of business strategy is given without referring to knowledge at all. After that the properties of knowledge are being discussed and why they could be valuable to a business strategy. Third the link between knowledge and strategy is being made based on where there are matching features.
After this introductory phase, Zack comes up with his knowledge strategy framework. In this framework different companies in different roles are being compared and different strategies they conduct. This is done by grouping companies on whether they sell products or services on one hand and whether they are physical or knowledge based on the other hand. The insights in this companies lead to a linking of a knowledge gap and a strategic gap.
Finally Zack devotes a paragraph to comparing a specific company to other companies in the same industry and the consequences that has for the strategy needed to be taken. The bottom line here is that companies don't stand on their own and that the shape of the competition impacts the way a company should handle.


I think his method is carefully thought of and the main point of his method is his framework. By explaining knowledge and business strategy first and showing what they can mean to each other it becomes clear why a framework could be useful at all. The framework links the knowledge gap to the strategic gap. The framework isn't a far fetched solution since it's closely linked to the current theory of SWOT analysis. He also clearly explains why the five companies are chosen, but one could consider if the findings of the companies could be generalized tot every company of that certain kind. One thing the writer could've added is alternatives to the currently chosen solution and similar efforts tried before on this area.

Next the key elements of his argument. These are basically the results of the method followed in the last two paragraphs. First his view on business strategy. When you want to be ahead of your competitors, you need to do something unique. The key term being mentioned here is a resource based view, which states that one should focus on the qualities it has to offer for sale instead of the products one offers. This is followed by the properties of knowledge being hard to copy, being intangible and hard to imitate which of course relates very much to the business strategy. Besides that Zack explains that the more a company knows, the more knowledge it can absorb. The link between the business strategy and knowledge is achieved by defining three kinds of knowledge resp. core, advanced and innovate knowledge. This ables someone to map knowledge strategically. About the link Zack literally says: “Every strategic position is linked to some set of intellectual resources and capabilities.” which shows potential tight bounds with knowledge. A concept arising here is a knowledge gap. This is the difference between what a company is able to do and should be able to do, which will be dealt with in the framework.
The framework introduces the strategic gap and links it to the knowledge gap. The strategy can be applied in two dimensions. One dimension states whether the organization is mostly a knowledge user versus a creator while on the other dimension the sources of the primary knowledge are listed being most internal versus external. Combining these dimensions leads to a new dimension in which scoring high on both dimensions classifies an organization aggressive and low on both as conservative in the most extreme cases. In order to illustrate the differences among the dimensions the five companies that were mentioned before are placed on them and being evaluated.
Finally the paragraph called industry learning cycles places a single company in the industry of its kind. Depending on the strategy of the competitors and the knowledge needed in the market, different industries justify difference knowledge strategies. After one has investigated its industry the choice for a certain knowledge strategy should be straightforward using the framework.


Now my critical assessment on the previous paragraph. The author shows clearly that in business strategy one needs to be and do something unique to be and stay ahead of the competition and that knowledge could play a key role in it. The author doesn't name alternatives that have been mentioned to fulfill this position by other people in the same field and therefore it's not necessarily convincing that knowledge is the only answer to stay ahead of competition. When Zack introduces his three kinds of knowledge depending on its importance being core, advanced and innovative, it's not exactly clear why to specifically use this trichotomy. What he does mention about this in advance is that he's seen knowledge of many different companies that would all fit in this categorization. After that the actual framework is being introduced and it's clear why he uses two dimensions to respect the currently available SWOT theory. 
What is a little unclear is the ordering of the three values on the dimensions, since unbound knowledge can imply both internal and external knowledge and it's not clear where a company would be in the framework if it for example uses moderate internal and moderate external knowledge. The same counts for the other dimension in which exploiter and explorer are both being covered by innovator.
Now what just mentioned can be use with respect to the competition. Zack clearly describes with the help of examples what needs to be done to fit in the market and also takes some time to illustrate that choosing a certain way can change an organization dramatically. 
Finally it's worth to take a moment and consider if Zack mainly propagates an objectivist or practice-based perspective on knowledge. This is not completely clear from the text and for either point of view one could give some arguments. First him being an objectivist: In his paragraph about industry learning cycles he mentions that knowledge can leak into the industry and be observed by other companies. This is impossible with tacit knowledge. On the other hand, when he mentions the unique properties of knowledge and it being hard to imitate in the knowledge as strategic resource paragraph one tends to believe he stands for practice-based. However, generally Zack has left this issue in the middle and for his framework and thoughts about this, it's not necessary to know it, since it can be applied in both situations while his framework is generic on this point.

Finally the relevance of the paper is being addressed. Normally one would expect to find what's achieved on this area in a paragraph called future work or in the conclusion, but in this paper this is not the case. What can be done is to take a look at the original objectives and the problems that are there in the field and in what way the paper has offered a solution for them. From scientific point of view Zack has offered a framework and taxonomy in which future work can be positioned. However, his way of work hasn't been literally described in the paper and therefore it can be hard to justify all the choices he has made.
From societal point of view the objectives play a role. The paper could  be helpful for organizations to asses their knowledge gap and what needs to be done in a certain industry to elaborate the right knowledge strategy. The framework makes it possible for organizations to say something sense about their current strategy. Also the paper could make organizations realize to see knowledge as their distinctive feature and how to exploit this.

Although the paper provides useful information it's a shame that Zack hasn't put more emphasis on explaining what this paper and his work has finally contributed. Implicitly he mentions this by addressing the problems in the beginning of the paper, but due to the lack of reflection one can't clearly determine in what way his framework has solved or dealt with them. The paper however does have relevance for the field, especially since similar work hasn't been done before and also because everything Zack deals with is linked to previous literature about similar topics.
