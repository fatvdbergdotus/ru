# History of Blu-ray

The history of the Blu-ray disc starts in 1982 when a need to store information digitally (in that case audio) was fulfilled. Philips and Sony developed the Compact Disc (CD), which is even nowadays a commonly used standard for storing audio.

Later the Compact Disc was used for storing digital information as well, called CD-ROM (read-only memory), and in the late 1990s consumers were able to record their own Compact Discs on a CD-R (recordable) disc using a CD burner.

In the 1990s the CD format was upgraded by using a more advanced laser and smaller spaces between the data on the disc. This technology is called DVD and the physical size of a DVD disc is the same as a CD. The storage size however increased from 700 megabytes to 9 gigabytes, which means more than 10 times as much space.

The initial goal of the DVD (Digital Video Disc) was to store video digitally, but shortly after that it was used for data as well and therefore the name Digital Versatile Disc was used. DVD was followed by HD DVD and Blu-ray, of which Blu-ray became the standard.

Blu-ray is more and more commonly used to store video in even higher quality than DVD. This is possible because of the storage capacity of 30 gigabytes.

At the same time, alternative storage technologies and internet distribution remain strong competitors.
