Below it shows three assignments made by Freek van den Berg for the course Strategy and Marketing in the context of the Computer Science Master.

## Assignment 1: The viability of Blu-ray
Blu-ray technology evolved from CDs and DVDs as a higher-capacity digital storage format designed mainly for high-definition video. The paper explains that Blu-ray became the industry standard after defeating HD DVD, thanks to its larger storage capacity, backwards compatibility, and growing adoption in computers and entertainment devices. It is considered a high-tech product because of uncertainties regarding future applications, competing technologies, and market developments. Key stakeholders such as consumer electronics manufacturers, motion picture companies, and computer hardware producers strongly influence Blu-ray’s design and adoption by demanding affordability, portability, compatibility, and high storage capacity. Although Blu-ray offers many advantages and has gained significant market acceptance, alternative technologies like streaming services, USB storage, and hard drives may challenge its long-term dominance.

## Assignment 2: Customer relation and marketing stragegies
This paper compares the views of Grönroos and Mohr on customer relations and marketing strategies. Grönroos argues that modern marketing should focus on building long-term relationships with customers, understanding their individual needs, and integrating marketing into every customer interaction rather than relying on the traditional 4 P’s and product-focused transactions. Mohr, in contrast, takes a broader and more financially oriented approach by emphasizing customer acquisition, segmentation, switching costs, pricing strategies, and profitability. He categorizes customers into groups such as true friends, butterflies, barnacles, and strangers to evaluate their value to the company. While Grönroos believes that satisfied and loyal customers naturally lead to profitability, Mohr points out that not all relationship-oriented customers are profitable and that even short-term customers can generate significant revenue. Overall, Grönroos presents a more specialized relationship-focused perspective, whereas Mohr provides a more general framework that balances customer relationships with financial outcomes.

## Assignment 3: Market, product, technology and plan
This essay examines how successful strategies can be developed in high-technology companies by combining the theories of Albright and Mohr. Albright’s roadmap framework consists of four connected elements—market, product, technology, and plan—which together guide companies in aligning customer needs, technological capabilities, and strategic planning. Mohr complements this by emphasizing continuous development processes and the importance of integrating products and services. The paper combines these ideas into a new framework in which each roadmap element must address both product and service dimensions, ensuring that technology, services, and customer needs remain aligned. This integrated theory is then applied to optical storage media such as CD, DVD, and Blu-ray, showing how market segments, supporting services, technologies, and strategic partnerships can be planned together. The essay concludes that high-tech companies should continuously update their roadmaps while combining product and service strategies to create complete and adaptable solutions for customers.
