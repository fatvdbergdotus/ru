# Introduction

This essay deals with developing successful strategies in high-technology companies.

In order to get an idea, the theory of roadmaps in Albright's corresponding paper is explained and extended by Mohr's view on products and services. Albright's paper is validated by using a different part of Mohr's book. The aspects from both theories are combined into a new one. After this, the new theory is applied to a product.

# Literature

The paper of Albright starts with stating that a roadmap should consist of four elements:

1. Market
2. Product
3. Technology
4. Plan

The different elements are processed in sequence by linking the result of one element to the next element. Linking back is also possible when different opportunities arise in other elements.

## Market Element

In the market element the positioning of the firm in the market is the central issue and what customers this may lead to.

This involves:

- Estimating what the market will do
- Determining what customer segments to target
- Analyzing competitors

Porter’s Five Forces can be useful for estimating market developments.

## Product Element

The product element describes what is possible and needed with respect to the product.

Important concepts include:

- Product drivers
- Metrics for measuring capabilities
- Delivery time and price metrics
- Trend lines for future functionality
- Evolution plans for future versions

## Technology Element

In order to realize a certain product, a certain level of technology is needed.

Technology is normally built in a modular way, and for every module the following can be analyzed:

- Historical development
- Current development
- Future development

## Plan Element

The plan element links all previously mentioned content together while also addressing risks.

This includes:

- Strategic summaries
- Risk identification
- Backup plans
- Future direction of the firm

# Mohr’s Perspective

In chapter 7 of Mohr’s book a similar approach is taken, but instead of delivering a roadmap document, a repeating cycle is run in which everything needed and wanted is constantly updated.

Mohr therefore focuses more on the process than on the deliverables.

Mohr also distinguishes four kinds of deliverables to the customer by combining the dimensions high/low-tech and product/service.

| Category | Advice |
|---|---|
| High-tech products | Extend the product with services such as consulting, training, repair and maintenance. |
| High-tech services | Provide a platform (product) that supports the service. |
| Low-tech products | Apply new technologies to improve customer service and logistics. |
| Low-tech services | Apply self-service technologies or innovate into high-tech services. |

# New Theory

Both theories have different focuses and perspectives, but they complement each other.

Where Albright focuses on connecting technology and market through different roadmap steps, Mohr distinguishes between products and services, stating that every product can be complemented with a service and vice versa.

This leads to a new framework in which the four elements:

- Market
- Product
- Technology
- Plan

are combined with an additional dimension:

- Product versus Service

This means that all four roadmap steps must be performed for both the product and the service that a company wants to deliver.

For example:

- If software is delivered as a product, suitable hardware must exist to run it.
- The programming language used must be compatible with the infrastructure.

This approach gives the customer a complete package of products and services.

The roadmap creation process can also be iterative and continuously updated, as suggested by Mohr.

# Product Example: Optical Storage Medium

The optical storage medium currently consists of:

- CD
- DVD
- Blu-ray

These technologies make it possible to store large amounts of information on discs using lasers.

Since research and development continue, Blu-ray is likely not the final platform in this product category.

The four roadmap elements are therefore applied below.

---

# Market Product

Different customer segments can benefit from optical storage media:

| Segment | Needs |
|---|---|
| Motion entertainment watchers | High-quality movie watching at home |
| Gamers | Storage for large and complex games |
| Professionals | Reliable, portable and cheap data transport and backup |

---

# Market Service

Services derived from the product include:

| Segment | Service |
|---|---|
| Motion entertainment watchers | Fast movie delivery services and high-quality movie catalogs |
| Gamers | Availability of advanced game titles |
| Professionals | Automatic backup systems and disc indexing/search capabilities |

---

# Product Product

Possible products include:

- A standalone player connected to TVs or LCD screens
- An OEM player for computers or game consoles
- A portable player connected through USB

---

# Product Service

The services identified in the market service section should be translated into concrete service offerings.

---

# Technology Product

Required technologies include:

- A laser capable of reading very small areas
- An energy-efficient electromotor
- A strong metal housing to protect the drive

In addition, an overview is needed of technologies used in CD, DVD and Blu-ray, together with future requirements.

---

# Technology Service

Required supporting services include:

- An internet website for communication
- Supply chain management systems
- Software supporting the hardware

---

# Plan Product

The product plan should:

- Connect all previous requirements
- Develop technology in modules
- Identify risks and backup actions

---

# Plan Service

The service plan should:

- Connect all previous requirements
- Find strategic partners
- Identify risks and backup actions

Examples of partners include:

- Game companies
- Movie distributors
- Software suppliers

---

# Continuous Development

Once the roadmap document has been created, it must continuously be enforced during development.

The document should also be updated periodically depending on:

- Progress made
- New technological insights
- Market developments

---

# Conclusion

Developing strategies in high-tech companies has been investigated using two complementary theories.

Albright identified a roadmap consisting of four elements, while Mohr focused more on the continuous process and the distinction between products and services.

Combining these theories led to an approach in which:

- The four roadmap elements are maintained
- Both product and service dimensions are integrated
- Alignment between product and service is required for every element

The new theory was applied to optical storage media and the successor of Blu-ray as an example.

This resulted in a brief development strategy and roadmap.
