# Introduction

In this paper the topic customer relations is addressed in the field of marketing. This is done by comparing what has been told in the paper *Relationship Marketing: Challenges for the Organization* by Grönroos with chapter three in the book *Marketing of High-Technology Products and Innovations* by Mohr (second edition).

The focus will mostly be on the differences in opinion, because this is the area in which most of the current discussion and uncertainty is taking place.

---

# The Comparison

Grönroos' paper provides a framework consisting of eight topics that are relevant in customer relations, while Mohr's book deals with the two topics customer acquisition and retention.

The framework Grönroos offers challenges the original 4 P's of marketing. While the 4 P's served pretty well as markers for what was going on with marketing, they have turned out to be too simplistic for modern marketing nowadays. This is a result of the following points among others:

- Customers do not just want to buy one single product. They want to be helped and advised with a certain need and would like to build up a relationship with a company to fulfill it.

- Quantitative analysis (which is often focused on the amount of profit and customers) does not work well anymore, because customers are not homogeneous. Success should be monitored with all the contact the company has with the customer.

- Marketing is not a separate entity in the company anymore, but happens with every single customer contact.

Above statements lead to an approach in which transactions and products are abandoned and in which a relationship with the customer and providing a resource for its need is preferred.

The downside of this could be that making money is not always the first priority and that short-term customers from which a company can make a lot of money are ignored.

This becomes clear when the chapter of Mohr's book is compared with the just mentioned. Mohr starts with describing how to acquire customers, which is done by illustrating some key points:

- **Segmentation** in which groups of customers with similar needs are clustered in the marketing system and approached the same way is the first step. This contradicts the approach of Grönroos who prefers a completely individualistic approach, but is still better than the traditional approach in which every customer buying a certain product is treated equally.

- **Switching costs** are another way. When customers have to make a lot of effort to go to a competing company with their needs, they are likely to stay. Grönroos however seems to prefer to only have customers that really want your services and focuses more on satisfying the customer.

- **Awareness generation** is mentioned next, which is described as communicating to your customers what you have to offer and setting a certain expectation level, for example through targeted email. Grönroos does not mention this topic too much, but does state that finding out what your customers need is very important.

- **Pricing and trial versions** of products are ways to lure customers into your company according to Mohr. Grönroos however does not mention these things at all, because helping the customer should be the first priority and when this goal is met, financial benefits are just a natural consequence.

# Customer Relationship and Retention Strategies

With respect to customer relationship and retention strategies, Mohr identifies four kinds of customers that with some imagination can be placed in Grönroos' two-dimensional approach, but need an extra dimension to state how much money they are willing to spend.

| Customer Type | Description |
|---|---|
| True friends | Long-lasting customers that can make a company a lot of money. In Grönroos' framework these would be relationship-resource based customers willing to spend a lot of money. |
| Butterflies | Short-term customers that make a lot of money. They buy many products for a short period and then move to another company. |
| Barnacles | Customers that want a relationship with the company and like to be helped well, but are not willing to spend much money. |
| Strangers | Transaction-oriented customers who only need a certain kind of product, similar to the traditional marketing approach mentioned by Grönroos. |

---

# Some Remarks About the Comparison

The two dimensions introduced by Grönroos and the additional money dimension implicitly mentioned by Mohr make it possible to identify which kind of customers both authors focus on.

For Grönroos these are customers that want a relationship and are resource-based. This is independent of the amount of money they can produce for the company, with the assumption that when a customer is helped well, they are always profitable.

For Mohr this kind of customer relationship is not necessarily profitable (see Barnacles) and additionally customers that pass by quickly can also be profitable. This is the case because they do not expect much personal attention from the company (Strangers) or buy more impulsively for a short period (Butterflies).

What can be concluded from the above is that Grönroos has an approach for a more specific group of customers and can therefore more clearly state what needs to be done with respect to marketing.

Mohr however focuses on all kinds of customer groups and marketing approaches, which results in giving the financial aspect more importance.

The reason Grönroos focuses on a certain style of marketing is because the world is changing and this approach can be applied to more and more products and perhaps eventually to every kind of customer and product.

# Conclusion

This paper compared a paper by Grönroos with a chapter from a book by Mohr with respect to customer relations and focused on the differences.

Although there seems to be overlap in what they state, both writers turned out to have different perspectives on customer relations.

While Grönroos focuses on making customers happy and keeping them closely connected to the company, Mohr focuses on getting customers to buy products first and then determining how to make money from them.

Grönroos notices changes in the economy and customer expectations and sees this relationship-oriented approach as the new way forward.

Mohr, on the other hand, provides an overview of different types of customers and whether they can be profitable. This includes Grönroos' type of customer, but also points out that such customers are not necessarily profitable.

Therefore Mohr's theory can be applied more generally, while Grönroos' theory is more specific and detailed.
