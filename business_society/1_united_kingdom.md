# Business and society: Country essay United Kingdom
September 18th, 2009

This essay will deal with the question 'How is economic life in the United Kingdom organized and regulated?'. In order to answer this question the framework of the course Business and Society of the Radboud University in Nijmegen and literature from the book 'Creating Modern Capitalism' by McCraw will be used. The frameworks offers eight themes, of which each theme uses the a differentiation in eights parts.

In order to determine how the British economic evolved to what it is now, it is important to take the history into account. Three industrial revolutions can be taken into account in doing so. In the first revolution the factory was introduced, which made it possible to people to just sell the labour, instead of the result product of the labor. Wedgwood was one of the entrepeneurs here with his pottery production and made it later possible for other individuals to set up a brand. This also made it possible to set up big organizations which worked very structurally compared to individual craftmanship, because labor was more organized. The efficiency was supported by privately financed infrastruce, such as canals and roads. This affected the organization and labor theme. 

In order to achieve this a market system had to be developed. This market system that was developed  made it possible to borrow money, to pay in bounds and have private ownership in the capital theme. Also contracts were legally enforced, so that companies were able to work together more without having too much initial trust. The market system was extended around the 1840s with a completely free trade which made it possible to import and export as much as one wishes. The downside of this turned out to be dependence on international trades a lot, which has resulted in a lot of wars in the British colony countries. As a result of the import and export regulations, technology and market became equal to everyone, instead of royal people ruling the country. This made London the center of trading and made it that many properties we see in capitalism were first set in the United Kingdom. Also the food industry benefited from the globalisation, which made it possible to deal with the intensively growing population and resulted in less and less people working in agriculture.

In order to counterfeit horrible working conditions, such as in Wedgwood's era in the first industrial revolution, trade unions arose, continuing in more power for socialism in the third revolution. This had its effect on the labor theme and resulted in more workable labor conditions. The improvement in working conditions was visible by forbidding child laber and limiting the working hours for adults.

What can be concluded from above, is that the United Kingdom has made the first steps in capitalism and embraced a system in which free trade and equality for everyone became possible. In order to guarantee decent working conditions, socialism is part of the country as well but in moderate form. There is no or hardly and power in the hands of the royal people anymore. The legal system in Britain supports capitalism by respecting contracts and making it possible to privately own things.
