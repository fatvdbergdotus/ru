## Introduction
This essay will deal with the question 'How is economic life in Germany organized and regulated?'. In order to answer this question the framework of the course Business and Society of the Radboud University in Nijmegen and literature from the book 'Creating Modern Capitalism' by McCraw will be used. The frameworks offers eight themes, of which each theme uses the a differentiation in eights parts.

## The shape of the country
The first remarkable part when analyzing Germany, is that over time the country has been very different. This is expressed by the physical borders of the countries over time, but also by the constant struggle to find some balance among the different states it consist of, even until current day. Over time this resulted among others in Zollverein (1830-1874), Weimar republic (1919-1933), The BRD and DDR (1949-1990) and finally after 1990 a reunification leaving one single country consisting of  16 states. With respect to the market this often meant that trading was very hard because of the many borders that had to be crossed inside the country.

## Different themes
Looking at the labor and technology part of Germany over time, one can see that the industrial revolution in Germany started very late. Because of this it is hard to distinguish the different phases of the different industrial revolutions. An advanced form of science (which was backed up by a school system in which virtually everyone attended) and banks led to advanced industries in which the share of skilled workers was very high. The managers and entepreneurs were often scientists.  Capital therefore really supported the labor and technology theme with Frankfurt still being the center of banking in Europe nowadays.

The capital that was created in Germany stayed mostly inside the country, due to the government protecting the inland market and being very involved in everything that happened in the country. In addition to this cartels between companies existed frequently. As a result of this Germany didn't or hardly had any colonies and was focused very inwards in the market theme. Only larges firms focused on export. The international relations theme is therefore hardly covered and even current day the German country is very self supporting by having a huge industry.

## Two germany companies
McCraw's book mentions two companies to illustrate Germany, which are August Thyssen with his steal company and the Deutsche bank, being one of the biggest banks in the world. The steal company illustrates well how the labor, organization, market, entrepeneur and technology theme were covered in Germany. This is expressed in the following properties of the steel company:
- The company was funded by banks
- Thyssen followed a high level education
- Thyssen was a scientist as well as a manager
- August Thyssen always focused on innovations and having the highest level technology. This is expressed by him listening to his junior managers
By making new companies all the time Thyssen avoided being involved too much in cartels. This was typically a-German. 


Next the Deutsche bank. The bank has set up a credit system, which for a big part shaped the market and capital theme of the country as it is nowadays. The bank gained a lot of influence in the industry, which was very normal in Germany at that time. The bank was under control of the government a lot, which illustrates the government power of Germany opposed to countries like the United Kingdom and United States. The Deutsch bank offered short term accounts for the inland market, so that companies could always lend a bit of money when being short and get a bit of reward in form of interest when having too much money. The bank in addition to this supported the exports of the big companies, making it a global player since the first world war already.

## Conclusion
This short paper investigated how the economic life in Germany is organized and regulated. This resulted into Germany being very late with the industrial revolution. Once the revolution started however, Germany was ahead of other countries in science, education and banking. This resulted into big, structured and advanced companies. Because of the market conditions and the government's influence these companies were mostly inland oriented, with the excepion of big companies exporting. The Thyssen steel company and the Deutsche bank company have been investigated and stated to be typical German companies. For Thyssen this is expressed in it's capital system, the highly skilled workers and being a big company. For the Deutsche bank the government influence is big and the company shaped much of what Germany is like now with respect to the market and capital theme.
