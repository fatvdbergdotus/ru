## Introduction
This essay will deal with the question 'How is economic life in the USA organized and regulated?'. In order to answer this question the framework of the course Business and Society of the Radboud University in Nijmegen and literature from the book 'Creating Modern Capitalism' by McCraw will be used. The frameworks offers eight themes, of which each theme uses the a differentiation in eights parts.

## Small Government
What is remarkable about the system in the USA, is that the role of the government is kept as small as possible. This has an affect on different other themes:
- Labor: It becomes easy to hire and fire people. The advantage of this is that companies can respond to market demands quickly. The disadvantage however is that an employee should always fear losing his job.
- Entepreneurship and capital: New ideas are very welcomed and starting a new company is relatively easy. Using joint ventures and other constructions it becomes easy to try a new idea and fail. Henry Ford is a good example here, since it took him several attempts to start a company that's successful. Along with this bankruptcy is seen as something unavoidable and practically waved away with respect to creditors. This means the rights of creditors a relatively limited, but whenever a creditor invests the profit can be very huge.
- Market and organization: The government is hardly involved at the market. This means low taxes and avoiding subsidies as much as possible. There have been some cases however in which one organization (such as Microsoft and AT&T) got too big and was able to use its near monopoly position. In these cases legal lawsuits, mostly initiated by the government were a logical result. Having very big firms is seen as something being typically American, which is paradoxial to the individual spirit.
For the government itself this means that its involvement should be as a little as possible and that the government should only be concerned about setting up the primary conditions for having a wealthy country, which means a market that functions as optimally as possible.

## Consequences for entrepreneurship
As just mentioned, new ideas are welcomed very much in the USA and starting a new company is relatively a piece of cake. As a result of this USA is mostly a country that is good at radical innovations, opposed to incremental innovations. Examples of companies from the USA that started a completely new market at their time are Google, Microsoft and Amazon.
Because people can be hired and fired easily, a lot of pressure on the employee occurs. It does however mean that people have to show the best of themselves, because competition is killing. This goes hand in hand with the American philosophy, in which individuals are seen as being more important then structures of the whole. The individual spirit can been seen in the unequal division of wealth, which means the the differences in income in the USA a very big.

## Conclusion
It turns out that many things that happen in the USA to regulate the country can be traced back to the individual, entrepeneurial spirit and a government that shows as little involvement as possible. This has resulted into leaving most of what happens to the market and giving individuals the chance to express their new ideas. Companies can reply to market demands quickly, because of low taxes and the ability to hire and fire people easily.
