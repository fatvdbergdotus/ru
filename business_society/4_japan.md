## Introduction
This essay will deal with the question 'How is economic life in Japan organized and regulated?'. In order to answer this question the framework of the course Business and Society of the Radboud University in Nijmegen and literature from the book 'Creating Modern Capitalism' by McCraw will be used. The frameworks offers eight themes, of which each theme uses the a differentiation in eights parts.
## Market
With respect to organizations acting on the market two kinds of institutions can be defined, namely the Zaibatsu and Keiretsu. The Zaibatsu is a structure in which a holding structure occurs in which everything is owned by a family. After loosing the second world war this structure was taken away by the USA, but continued later in the form of a Keiretsu. The Keiretsu developed as loosely connected business groups.  A Keiretsu only had one business per sector to avoid competition and every business in the Keiretsu preffered to buy from each other to support each other. Because of the just mentioned, direct competition is often avoided and helping each other is promoted.
## Organization
For the organization the just mentioned led to organizations in which cooperating and sharing knowledge is the norm. Toyota is a good example here, by sharing the knowledge to its members and forcing them in return to share knowledge as well to stay in the Keiretsu. This is illustrate by Toyota's policy by offering processes to share knowledge in both dimensions, being explicit/tacit and multilateral/bilateral.  In addition to this the Japanese introduced lean concepts, in which waste (through stocks or materials that have to be thrown away) is marked to be the biggest sin.
## Technology and international trade
The knowledge sharing and open structure has led to Japan being highly innovative and doing very well in exporting through its highly competeting position. Adding the minimization of waste, which led to competitive prices. Both these aspect have led to a very positive trading balance of Japan, meaning that much more leaves the country then enters.
## Capital
The way Keiretsu were set up, lead to the banks being in the middle of the conglomerate. This made it possible to deal with debt and credit quickly and efficiently, since the different members of the Keiretsu could balance each other out.
## Labor
Another consequence of the organizational structure is that eliminations of waste included the firing of workers as well. This lead to high pressure of being fired, since only the best were allowed to stay. There were different attempts to establish unions of workers, but they all failed. This led to the power being in the hands of the companies and resulted in a 'hire and fire' culture, similar to the USA.
## Entrepeneur
Due to its imitation of knowledge that has already been there and improving it, Japanese company have often been using a leapfroggring strategy. This means coming from behind and then catching up and overtaking the market leaders by looking at what they are doing and improving it. This made small businness able to grow very big and thus gave entrepeneurs big chances to become market leaders.
## Conclusion
Japan is a country in which congloromates of companies named Keiretsu are very common. The companies cooperate and share knowledge, which benefits them. In addition to this lean production is used, leading to a very competitive market position. Banks are in the center of the congloromates. Workers are not protected well, making the companies very flexible and entrepeneurship is very much possible due to Japan's leapfrogging strategy.
